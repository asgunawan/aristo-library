# Swiss Escape And Bern Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the specific sequence:

### Ohio -> Kingdom -> Swiss storage unit -> Bern Federal Palace

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to make the chain feel coherent, tense, and usable.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- Aiden escapes from a watched apartment using a concealed emergency anchor
- the U.S. side witnesses only the departure anomaly
- he relays through the kingdom because that is where the mana and stable transit logic live
- he arrives in a Swiss storage unit with enough reserves to function, but not enough reason to act theatrical
- he goes to the Federal Palace perimeter in field-kit mode
- he approaches as a serious but still deniable stranger, not as a threat
- the first proof is small, near-free, and impossible to fake, but not spectacle
- the guard escorts him indoors instead of dismissing him
- the scene becomes semi-public only because reality leaks through phones, confusion, and proximity

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. Ohio: the walls closing in

Aiden is back in his apartment, but it no longer feels like home.

He knows:
- he is being watched
- his devices are compromised
- his money and purchases have become a pattern
- whatever happens next will not be interpreted in his favor

This matters because he is not leaving on a whim.

He is leaving at the point where the ordinary American version of himself is about to become procedurally unrecoverable.

## 2. The hidden anchor

He pulls back the rug.

Under it is the emergency circle he painted earlier.

This is not a beautiful ceremonial object.
It is a practical panic exit.

That detail helps the scene.

It says:
- he planned for a bad day
- he hoped not to need it
- now he needs it

He teleports in what he is wearing: plain civilian clothes.
His gear lives on the kingdom side, in the prepared bag.

## 3. What the watchers see

The surveillance team does not get a full magical lecture.

They see something like:
- Aiden clearing the center of the room
- Aiden exposing some kind of geometric painted pattern
- Aiden stepping into it
- a ritualized verbal or physical sequence they cannot categorize
- then a flash that blows out sensors
- then an empty room

The image they are left with is not "wizard confirmed."

It is:

### something impossible just happened in a room we thought we controlled.

That is enough.

## 4. Kingdom relay

He does not go straight from Ohio to Bern.

He lands first in a known kingdom-side anchor point.

This does several jobs at once:
- gives the magic system internal logic
- explains where the real mana comes from
- gives him a moment to reset, grab reserves, grab the prepared bag, and commit
- keeps Earth from feeling too easy to work around

This is a mana and reset beat, not a suiting-up beat.

The identity transition happens later, in Switzerland.

Emotionally, this beat is useful too.

For a moment, he is back in the world where he actually knows who he is.
Then he has to leave again.

## 5. The Swiss jump

From the kingdom side, he performs the harder long-range search toward the Swiss storage unit anchor.

He has:
- address details
- images of the room
- confirmation the symbol is intact
- enough mana to make the search-lock plausible

He arrives in Switzerland physically strained, but not wrecked.

Important point:

### he is not arriving there to start a fight.

He is arriving there to change categories.

## 6. The storage unit pause

This pause matters.

He does not step into Bern already performing myth.

He uses the storage unit to become legible.

This is where he:
- changes into field-kit mode
- puts on the hero robe with the short mantle
- checks crystals and proof items
- steadies himself
- rehearses the first sentence in his head

This is the transition from:

### terrified student fleeing containment

to:

### representative attempting recognition.

## 7. The taxi

He has just done something impossible.
Then he has to do something embarrassingly normal.

That tonal drop is not a flaw.
It is one of the story's strengths.

The taxi driver is not a plot witness or a secret helper.
He is an ordinary Bern cabbie, and he half-deflates the myth.

Rough beat:

- the driver jokes: is this a costume party, are you a tourist, what is in the bag
- Aiden answers lightly, tired, halfway between identities
- on arrival at the drop point the driver asks for a photo because the robe is cool
- Aiden lets him
- he pays with a generous tip
- the driver says something like don't do anything stupid
- Aiden says he won't
- he will

Keep the driver grounded.
He exists to make the whole thing feel real and slightly ridiculous.

## 8. Approaching the Bundeshaus

Briefly describe the Federal Palace for the reader:
- tourists moving, filming, eating
- the plaza broad and open, the building's green dome above it
- ordinary civic life, not a fortress

Aiden's interior: nervous, tired, and quietly regretting he did not eat first.

He scans the area, picks his officer, and walks toward him with purpose.
He is already being eyed and filmed by tourists.

Crucially: he does not glide, blink, or strut.

### He walks, because a man in a strange robe looks off in Bern regardless, and the weirdness should be the clothes, not the entrance.

## 9. The first contact

He drops the guard's nickname, then his full name.

The guard locks eyes, bewildered.

Aiden does not declare his own importance.
That is what every crank does, and every guard has heard it.

Instead he:
- names the supervisor's rank correctly
- drops one small, non-public, surface fact about the officer
- names the specific authority he needs to reach before it can be argued with

He lets the officer choose to escalate rather than be argued into believing.

This is the analyze beat, and it is kept shallow:
- a name, an age, an occupation, a small detail
- near-free, surface-only, and it reads as "this man did his homework," not yet "wizard"

### The guard's read is not belief. It is: this is above my pay grade, so I move him, not argue with him.

## 10. The escort, and the joke

Fedpol are protection officers, not investigators.

Their instinct with a weird-but-cooperative, non-aggressive stranger on camera is to move him off the plaza and assess him indoors, not to arrest him on the spot.

So the guard escorts him in, probing as they walk.

Then the joke, as a low-stakes management move:

- the guard asks about the robe
- Aiden answers flat and refuses to perform: it is an office, it matters to someone where he is from
- the guard, half-smiling: okay, magician, make this phone float
- Aiden does it, cheaply, and the guard laughs because so far it is still a box he can hold: good magician, at least this is entertaining

Keep the robe answer short.
Aiden should not lecture his clothing on camera.

## 11. The sidearm

This is the real escalation turn.

Unprompted, the loaded service weapon begins to drift a few centimeters out of its holster.

The guard slaps a hand down on it, grips it, and looks at Aiden like the joke just died.

One colleague sees. Maybe a second.

The important movement is small:
- a slow rise, a quiet return, as if Aiden were showing a cufflink

### Restraint reads as control. Control reads as "he could have done worse and chose not to."

That is what shatters the magician box.

He has not threatened anyone.
He has demonstrated capability, and capability that cannot be explained as hidden tech.

No battery, no string, no projector floats a stranger's own firearm.

## 12. Why this proof works

The proof is built to break the box, not to impress.

- analyze (shallow) shatters "crank": he knows things he should not
- telekinesis on the phone is cheap and keeps him in "good magician" just long enough
- the sidearm is the one thing Earth categorically cannot fake

Order matters:

### physical trick first gives them a box. Knowing things first leaves them no box.

So the small float lands as entertainment, and only then does the sidearm land as installation of fact.

## 13. Analyzed skill rules

This is the first public use of analyze, so it needs internal rules, even if only implied here.

- shallow read: name, age, occupation, obvious state; near-free, instant, surface-only
- deep read: private detail, hidden intent; costs mana, time, and proximity
- resistance scales with how guarded the target is, and with training

The rule that matters most here:

### Earth is mana-dead, so people on Earth are harder to read than people in the kingdom.

In the fantasy world everyone is soaked in ambient mana, so the surface layer is loud.
On Earth a person carries no mana, so Aiden must actively push mana into them just to touch the surface.

Reading one guard indoors is fine.
Reading many, or reading deep, is a real reserve cost he has to budget.

## 14. The jurisdiction hinge

The Federal Palace has a specific authority chain, and the scene leans on it.

- Kantonspolizei Bern own the plaza and everything outside the buildings
- Fedpol (blue uniforms, "POLICE fedpol" on the back) run guard and escort duty around the Parliament Building
- inside, the Parlamentsdienste are Hausherr of the Parliament Building itself

So Aiden is a cantonal problem walking toward a federal problem.

His entire move is to force the handoff across that boundary:
- from street nuisance
- to federal security matter
- to diplomatic classification

### The guard's office is not arbitrarily chosen. Dragging him inside is the jurisdictional crossing point.

## 15. The leak

The scene begins private in intention, but becomes semi-public in practice.

The leak is organic:
- tourists filming before any magic happens
- palace-adjacent foot traffic
- the taxi driver's photo
- the simple fact that a man in a hero robe approached a federal officer and was not arrested on the spot

He did not come to stage a concert.
He came to force recognition.

Reality makes that messy.

## 16. What changes after the proof

The important shift is not that everyone suddenly believes all his claims.

The shift is that the category changes.

Before proof, he is:
- maybe a nuisance
- maybe a crank
- maybe a security problem

After proof, he is:
- a containment problem
- a federal problem
- a diplomatic problem
- a strategic opportunity

That is the actual goal of the scene.

## What To Avoid

### 1. Immediate giant spectacle

That pushes him too early into "threat" instead of "problem that must be heard."

### 2. Full war-gear reveal before contact

That makes him look less like a petitioner and more like an invader.

### 3. The blink-step, the strut, the aura-farm

He should walk. The strange robe does the imposing. Bravado or speed reads as threat, and undercuts the diplomat he is trying to project.

### 4. A perfectly cooperative insider

There is none. The taxi driver is ordinary, the guard is skeptical, and nobody has been arranged to help.

### 5. Lecturing the robe or the magic too early

Explaining breaks the spell and gives the guard a box. Aiden shows capability, he does not perform a syllabus.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the Swiss pivot as a restrained escalation. Aiden should not arrive trying to dazzle the public. He should arrive trying to be moved indoors by the smallest layer of state authority that can still escalate him upward. Know the guard's name to prove he is not a crank, float the phone to stay momentarily harmless, then let the sidearm drift so the joke dies. The scene gets stronger when the spectacle leaks out of a controlled political move, instead of replacing it.

## Short Version

He should flee quietly, travel magically, arrive awkwardly, and prove himself surgically. The proof is not one big trick but a sequence: know something, show something small, then do the one thing Earth cannot explain. The scene rises not because anyone believes, but because the category he occupies stops being dismissible.

## Rough Flow In Plain Language

If I were summarizing the sequence very simply:

### Aiden flees a watched Ohio apartment through a concealed emergency circle, relays through the kingdom to use its mana and stable anchor network, arrives at a Swiss storage unit and changes into field-kit mode, takes a taxi into Bern and approaches the Federal Palace as a serious but still deniable stranger. The guard escorts him in as a probable crank, tests him with a joke, and watches his loaded sidearm rise a few centimeters on its own. The scene never begins as a public stunt, but it leaks into public visibility because a man in a hero robe approached a federal officer on camera and was not immediately arrested.
