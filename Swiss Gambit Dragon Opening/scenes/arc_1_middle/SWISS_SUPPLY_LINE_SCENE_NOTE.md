# Swiss Supply Line Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the third movement of Arc 1's middle (Phase 2: "maybe I can juggle this if I am clever").

### the storage depot -> the kingdom buff -> the cargo dock -> the sister's perspective -> the refugee foreshadow -> the mage's secret visit

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to show the double life *scaling up* — becoming a proper, almost-professional operation — right before the strain and the incursion that will crack it.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- Colin moves his whole operation out of the apartment and into a rented storage depot near his place — a covert base that reads normal at first glance
- the depot holds: a rug over the anchor circle (the cargo dock), boxes of Earth goods, and the gold jammed on a shelf (hidden-ish, not really)
- the kingdom buff runs through the ebook spreading (transcribed into copied books) plus physical templates bought from IKEA/Home Depot for the smiths to reverse-engineer
- the cargo dock mechanism: store -> move the rug -> dump goods on the circle -> ping the mage -> the mage pulls the cargo through WITHOUT Colin
- the sister's perspective hardens slightly: her own intern, a brother who is out a lot, sometimes in nice clothes ("a date? a side gig?"), who pays for things — light suspicion, still let go
- the refugee foreshadow: a small buffer kingdom collapses in a succession crisis (quietly rival-backed), and the wave of refugees begins to build on the frontier
- he accepts the "keep working part-time during the semester" offer, because he needs the money — which sets the collision for Callback 2
- the mage, reckless and curious, finds a flimsy excuse to teleport to the depot, explores, is weirded out by the dead air, cannot get out, and — having seen it — messages Colin as if nothing happened

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. The storage depot

Colin is not stupid, and the apartment was never a real base. So he moves.

A rented storage unit, near the apartment. His covert dock. At first glance it is normal: boxes, a few tools, a rug on the floor, a shelf of supplies.

Under the rug is the anchor circle.

On the shelf, the gold is just... jammed in among the boxes. Technically hidden, not really, because he is gambling — correctly, mostly — that nobody is auditing his storage unit.

Two things worth keeping in mind for later:
- it is rented under his real name, paid by card, on an access log — a mild trace, findable if someone goes looking, but not actively monitored
- this is where the CARGO teleport happens; the apartment anchor stays as his personal/emergency line (the one the closing escape uses)

## 2. The kingdom buff

The knowledge is spreading. The mage transcribes the ebook into copied books, and those books circulate.

But there is a category Colin can only half-help with: the things he can *state* but not *specify*.

- "filter the water through charcoal and sand" — yes, but not the layer depths or flow rate
- "build a pump" — but not the valve seat angle
- "make soap" — but the lye:fat ratio is exact
- "a truss holds the roof" — but not the load math
- "a better plow" — but not the measurements

For those, he buys the **physical templates** — a real hand-pump, a real filter, a real tool — from IKEA and Home Depot, and teleports them through for the smiths to reverse-engineer. Knowledge seed (the books) + physical template (the tools). That is the capability-seed model, running at full speed.

## 3. The cargo dock (the mechanism)

The routine is: Colin buys, Colin stores, Colin teleports.

- goods go into the car trunk, then the depot
- when a batch is ready: **move the rug, dump the goods on the circle, ping the mage on the mirror stone**
- the mage, kingdom-side, does the search-lock and **pulls the cargo through — without Colin**

The circle is a two-way shipping pad, and the mage does the actual transit (she is the teleporter; cargo costs by mass). Over the summer they have optimized it into a semi-consistent schedule, which is why the kingdom can count on the supply line.

## 4. The sister's perspective

The sister has her own intern now, at a different company.

Her read, in her own register:

Her brother goes out a lot. Sometimes in nice clothes — *a date?* He says it is a "side gig," but never quite lays out what. She doesn't push.

Because — whatever, you know? They still hang out. He still pays for things, thanks to the internship and the "side gig." So it's fine. It's probably fine.

The suspicion is light, and it is let go. That is the point: it is *real* but not *urgent*, and it will only become urgent later, when the operation stops being discreet.

## 5. The refugee foreshadow

On the kingdom side, something is quietly wrong — and it is the RANGER who brings it back, because the frontier is his beat.

He returns from a patrol and reports it, in his flat, ol'-reliable way:

The other kingdoms are reeling from the demon-army banditry too — except, strangely, they are not being attacked as often. And they are not accepting human refugees the way they did during the war.

And then the detail only a scout would catch: *"The bandits aren't hitting them the way they're hitting us. Something's wrong with that."*

Colin does not put the two facts together yet. But the reader can start to:

- the bandits are being *redirected*, away from the rivals and toward the kingdom
- the borders are *closing*, pushing the refugees toward the kingdom

Then the catalyst lands, reported the same way — **a small buffer kingdom on the frontier collapses into a succession crisis / brief civil war, quietly fomented by the rival coalition.** The wave of refugees begins.

It is not bad luck. It is the first visible move of the encirclement. But Colin just sees the refugee numbers climbing and the frontier lords starting to panic.

## 6. The internship decision

At the end of summer, the company offers to keep Colin on part-time during the school year.

He accepts. He needs the money — the supply line is running, and the kingdom's needs are only going up.

The acceptance is not the mistake. The mistake is what it sets up: **fall classes + part-time internship + the supply line + the gold + the kingdom, all at once.** Callback 2 will land exactly in that pile-up.

## 7. The mage's secret visit (the set piece)

This is the centerpiece of the note, and it is the mage's recklessness made physical.

### The flimsy excuse

Colin told her there would be ten piles of clothes in the last shipment. There are only nine in the kingdom.

So the mage decides — mostly to herself, entirely without needing to — that she *has* to teleport over and check.

> "Colin said ten piles. There are nine. I have to go look. (She does not.)"

She teleports to the depot.

### "Wow, I'm really in Colin's world!"

She lands inside the locked unit and is, for one beat, genuinely thrilled. She is standing in the other world. The place where Colin lives.

The air is a little stale in the closed unit, but whatever. That is not what's wrong.

What's wrong is the air itself — the weightless, dead, *unanswering* air. The same wrongness Colin felt the night he came home. She feels it for the first time from the other side, and it unsettles her in a way she can't name.

### The errand, then the curiosity

She finds the missing pile of clothes (the ninth and tenth, sitting in a box that never made the circle). She picks it up.

She is about to teleport back.

But she is curious — she is *always* curious — so she wanders the unit instead. The gold on the shelf. The lamp. The tools. Colin's whole secret, spread out in a storage unit, and her standing in the middle of it.

### Trapped

She cannot get out. The door is locked from the outside. She is, for a while, genuinely stuck in a small metal room on a dead world, and the wrong air is closing in.

It stops being exciting. It becomes small, and stale, and wrong.

### Back, and the casual message

Finally, tuckered out and thoroughly weirded out, she teleports back to the kingdom — the missing clothes in hand, because she is nothing if not thorough.

And then she messages Colin, as if the whole thing were a normal errand:

> "hey, I picked up the missing clothes pile. next time double check before letting me know, okay?"

## 8. The aftermath

Colin reads the message and freezes.

She was on Earth. She was in his storage unit. She walked through his secret and picked up his cargo and came back, and her entire reaction is *"double check next time."*

He does not know whether to be terrified or grateful or furious. Probably all three.

The mage does not understand why it is a big deal. It was just his world. The air was wrong. She went and came back. What is there to be upset about?

And underneath the panic, the note should land the quiet thing: **for the first time, someone from the kingdom has seen Colin's exile.** She has felt the dead air. She has stood in the small metal room that is, in some real way, what his whole Earth life is — and she came back calling it *stale* and *wrong* and acting like it was nothing.

He is not less alone. But now one other person knows what he has been walking through.

## Why This Works

This note does the quiet scaling-up that makes the collapse land:

- the storage depot makes Colin's operation *smarter* and *more real* — and gives it a concrete, findable location for later
- the kingdom buff shows the capability-seed model at full speed, without over-promising
- the cargo dock gives the supply line a mechanism, not a hand-wave
- the sister's perspective keeps the Earth-side cost alive without melodrama
- the refugee foreshadow plants the encirclement so Callback 3 has a motive
- the internship decision sets the collision for Callback 2
- the mage's visit is the emotional center: a reckless, curious incursion that mirrors Colin's own opening and confirms his exile rather than dissolving it

## What To Avoid

### 1. The mage's visit becoming a plot disaster

It is purely private — no trace. There are no cameras inside the unit (facility cameras are at the gate and hallways, not inside individual units), and she teleports in and out without ever opening the door, so she never crosses one. Nothing breaks. It is a character beat, not a plot turn. The mage goes, is weirded out, comes back, mentions it casually.

### 2. The mage understanding Earth

She does not. She feels the wrong air and calls it stale. She is fascinated and repelled at once, and she does not have the context to make sense of it.

### 3. Colin over-reacting into a fight

He is scared, not angry. The boundary gets set — "you can't just do that" — but it is a line, not a blowup. The mage keeps wanting to go back, and that tension is recurring, not resolved.

### 4. The kingdom buff turning into a montage

The books, the tools, the reverse-engineering — show it through one or two concrete beats (a smith puzzling over a hand-pump), not a list.

### 5. The refugee wave becoming the crisis yet

It is foreshadow. The numbers climb, the lords worry, but the cholera has not broken out. Keep it as an undertone.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the supply line as the moment the double life becomes almost professional — a storage depot, a cargo schedule, a kingdom running on his IKEA runs — and then puncture it with the mage's secret visit. Let her go, get weirded out by the dead air, and come back calling it "stale," and let Colin realize that someone has finally seen his exile. The scene succeeds when the operation feels competent and the incursion feels intimate, both at once.

Colin moves his operation to a storage depot (rug over the circle, gold on a shelf, normal at a glance) and runs the kingdom buff through transcribed books and reverse-engineered IKEA tools, teleported by the mage on a semi-consistent cargo schedule.
