# Swiss Trial And Error Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the second movement of Arc 1's middle (Phase 2: "maybe I can juggle this if I am clever").

### summer visit -> medicine + the ebook -> the "need money" realization -> the blacksmith AHA -> the first clean deal -> the routine

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to show the gold system being BORN — deliberately, for the kingdom's sake, not just for a birthday — and to settle Colin into the routine that Callback 2 will break.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- during summer break the kingdom calls again, medicine-related — a foreshadow of the refugee crisis, not yet a crisis: "people displaced by the demon army are gathering"
- Colin brings a medicine kit (and donuts); he, the healer, and the mage establish how Earth medicine works
- he leaves a tablet + solar/hand-crank charger + offline wiki — the "ebook" — and teaches the mage to use it
- the mage (curious, analytical) tries to dismantle it; analyze on it returns only knowledge-bounded guesses ("glass? metal? gift from Colin?")
- the visit exposes his real gap: common sense he already gave them, but the "needs internet / needs research" stuff is his hole
- the realization lands: he needs MONEY to buy supplies — water pump, sanitation tools, medicine (the bicycle dies on the rubber problem)
- he talks to the gold blacksmith -> the AHA -> the "low-quality, deliberately-inconsistent gold ring" strategy is born
- back on Earth he researches "real estate gold" content creators -> "yeah, I can maybe do this"
- the first CLEAN deal as the persona (the dealer senses he is new, but gold is gold)
- the normalization: the routine, the return cadence, the sister's worry, the society that notices but does not dig
- it ends on the false conclusion: "see, I can do both"

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. The summer visit (foreshadow, not crisis)

Summer break. The kingdom calls again — this time medicine-related.

It is not the refugee crisis yet. It is the early shape of it: **people displaced by the demon army are gathering in the frontier towns, and there is sickness in the crowding.**

Colin goes. He does not yet know this is the first tremor of the thing that will later need $20,000 of medicine. He treats it as a normal visit.

## 2. The medicine kit (establishing how it works)

He brings a medicine kit — and, because it is summer and he is comfortable, donuts.

The key beat is educational, not heroic: Colin, the healer, and the mage sit down and **establish how Earth medicine actually works**, versus magic.

The locked rules do the work here: potions mend wounds and tissue, but they do not kill bacteria or fix dehydration or stop infection. Earth medicine does. This is a *complement*, not a competition — which is why the healer is interested rather than threatened.

Colin browses his phone for offline wiki pages, shows them what he is reading, and the conversation lands on the offer:

### "I can probably leave you an ebook. Something to update the information when I'm not here."

## 3. The ebook (tablet + charger + wiki)

The ebook is a real object: **a tablet, a solar (or hand-crank) charger, and an offline wiki.**

Colin does this because it is the obvious move for him — the "teach a man to fish" move — and the only reason he did not do it sooner is that he did not think the problem would get this complicated. That retroactively covers the earlier absence.

He leaves a paper "how to use the ebook" manual, and teaches the mage.

## 4. The mage and the machine (the curiosity, the dismantling, the silicon)

The mage is the audience for this, and she is the right one: curious, analytical, precise.

She reads the manual. Then — of course — she tries to **dismantle the tablet** to understand it.

Two beats carry this:

### The analyze limit (a locked rule, made visible)

Analyze works on the user's own knowledge. So when the mage runs `analyze` on the tablet, it does not hand her the truth. It returns her own best guesses, filtered through what she already knows:

> "glass? metal? gift from Colin. and then a run of close estimations on how it works, probably."

She has no concept of silicon or transistors, so analyze has nothing to draw on. The skill is knowledge-bounded — it sharpens what the user understands, it does not import what they do not.

### The silicon comedy

She asks how the metal thing shows pictures with no magic, and Colin, trying to explain a computer to a mage, descends through the whole absurd stack:

> "Okay, so first you take a rock called silicon—"
> "And then you shine a light on it, a very specific kind of light—"
> "And then there's these 0 and 1 gates, using electricity—"
> "...and yes, you can rename the book if you press your finger down for a long time."

The collision is the joke: he reaches for the entire history of semiconductor manufacturing and lands on "long-press to rename." She is fascinated and completely uncomprehending at once.

## 5. The gap (common sense vs. research)

The visit exposes what Colin is actually worth, and it is not what he thought:

- the **common sense** stuff he already gave them, during his two years — sanitation, clean water, boil orders
- the **research / internet** stuff is the hole — the things you only know by looking them up, by studying, by cross-checking

That is his gap, and it is the thing he cannot just *remember* to them. He has to *go back and buy* the solution, because the knowledge is on a phone he cannot leave them and a supply chain he cannot carry.

## 6. The "need money" realization

The conclusion lands hard:

### "Damn. I need money to buy these."

The shopping list writes itself, and the bicycle is the object lesson:

- **bicycles** — he wants them for couriers and patrol, but rubber does not exist in this world (natural rubber is New World, synthetic is petrochemical). Wooden or iron wheels gut the point. The bicycle dies here, and he learns the rule: **the gifts that work need no exotic materials.**
- **hand-crank water pump** — pure mechanics, like an Archimedes screw but modern. Works.
- **sanitation tools** — shovels, soap, chlorine. Works.
- **medicine** — ORS, antibiotics. Works.

The pattern: mundane-but-scalable beats mythic-but-scarce. And mundane-but-scalable costs *money*.

## 7. The blacksmith AHA (the strategy is born)

Colin goes to the gold blacksmith and lays out the problem honestly — he needs money on the other side, and the kingdom's gold is the only thing he has to sell.

The AHA arrives in the conversation. The blacksmith, initially confused (why would a hero need *money*?), and Colin, working it out in real time, land on the strategy:

### Make low-quality, deliberately-inconsistent gold rings. Plain bands, no crests, no house marks, varied batch to batch — because a messy, inconsistent batch reads as "genuinely inherited over time," while a perfect uniform stack reads as manufactured.

This is the deliberate birth of the system that the Fumble only accidentally stumbled toward. The "never the weird stuff" lesson gets a plan attached to it.

## 8. The first clean deal (the persona)

Back on Earth, Colin researches. He watches "real estate gold" content creators — people who buy and flip inherited jewelry — and learns the shape of the lie.

> "Yeah. I can maybe do this."

Then he tries it. The dealer can tell this kid is new — the over-prepared cover story, the slight too-much confidence — but the gold is gold, the story holds, and the sale goes clean.

It is his first **clean** deal *as the persona*, distinct from the nervous fumble. The persona holds. That is the dangerous part: it *works*.

## 9. The normalization (the routine)

Now the double life settles into a rhythm, and this is where the old "Double Life Rhythm" texture lives:

- **return cadence:** he shifts from "per-call" to "proactive" — volunteering to come, but still through the mage's door (the impulse is his, the door is theirs)
- **party warmth:** a bandit-hunt, a short delve — the moments he is drifting toward
- **normal-life surface:** the group project (done, but with distance — "you're always busy, man"), the drifting year-4 friend, the internship grinding on
- **the sister's worry:** the backpack, the "hiking/camping," the phone that sometimes will not ring — called out lightly, and let go. "He's still my brother. He still treats me out. But what if he disappears again, and this time he never comes back?"
- **the society that notices but does not dig:** he is visible enough to be noticed, not important enough — yet — to be investigated. That is what keeps him safe now, and what the trace will later erase.

The routine is balanced, timed, almost elegant. Colin believes he has found the shape of the thing.

## 10. The false conclusion

The beat ends where it has to:

### "See? I can do both."

It is the lie, and it has to feel true here — or its collapse in Callback 2 will mean nothing.

## Why This Works

This is where the gold system stops being an accident and becomes a *plan*, and where the double life stops being a fumble and becomes a *rhythm*:

- the medicine visit foreshadows Callback 2 without being it
- the ebook + the dismantling + the silicon comedy show the collision of worlds at its most charming, and lock the analyze-is-knowledge-bounded rule in action
- the "need money" realization gives the gold system its real, kingdom-sized motive
- the blacksmith AHA makes the strategy deliberate instead of accidental
- the first clean deal proves the persona works, which is what makes it dangerous
- the routine and the false conclusion set up the exact thing Callback 2 will break

## What To Avoid

### 1. Making the medicine visit the full crisis

No. It is the foreshadow, not the crisis. The crowding is mentioned, not yet cholera. Keep it a normal visit with a wrong-feeling undertone.

### 2. Over-explaining the tablet

The silicon exchange is comedy, not a lecture. Cut it before it becomes a syllabus. The point is the collision, not the information.

### 3. Making the mage understand the tablet

She does not. That is the joke and the point. Fascination, not comprehension.

### 4. The bicycle as a working gift

It dies on the rubber problem, on purpose. It is the object lesson that teaches the "no exotic materials" rule.

### 5. A montage with no spine

The normalization should be felt scenes, not a list: one return, one party beat, one sister beat, one helpdesk beat.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the trial-and-error as the moment the gold system stops being an accident and becomes a plan. Land the medicine visit as a foreshadow, let the ebook and the mage's dismantling carry the world-collision comedy, and let the "damn, I need money" realization give the gold system its real motive. End on the routine and the false "I can do both" — because the whole point is that it feels true right up until Callback 2 snaps it.

## Short Version

On a summer visit, Colin brings a medicine kit and leaves a tablet-and-offline-wiki "ebook" for the mage, who promptly tries to dismantle it and runs analyze into a wall of her own ignorance ("glass? metal? gift from Colin?"). The visit exposes his real gap — the research-level knowledge he cannot carry — and the realization that he needs money for pumps, sanitation, and medicine. He works out the low-quality-gold-ring strategy with the blacksmith, studies "real estate gold" content creators, and lands his first clean deal as the persona. Then the double life settles into a routine, and he concludes, wrongly, that he can do both.
