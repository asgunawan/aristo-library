# Swiss Callback 2 Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the third movement of Arc 1's middle (the break of Phase 2: "maybe I can juggle this" collapses).

### the cholera crisis -> the $20k logistics chain -> the overdrive -> the trip wires -> the correlation -> the internship dies

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to make the second callback the moment the double life stops being clever and starts being visible.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- cholera breaks out in the frontier refugee camps, where Colin's sanitation never reached and the influx overwhelmed what did
- magic cannot scale to it: potions mend wounds, not disease; healers are scarce and treat one patient at a time
- the fix is modern and cheap but must be bought in BULK: ORS, antibiotics, water purification, IV supplies — roughly $20,000, fast
- the real killer is the logistics: buy in bulk, take delivery at his apartment, lie to his sister, teleport cargo through the kingdom mages, teach them to use it, verify it is used right
- the overdrive breaks his cadence: shop after shop, city after city, days in a row, spreading it out because he thinks geography is caution
- the trip wires fire: the LeadsOnline cluster (same face, high-purity gold, many shops, one week) and the structuring flag (cash deposited in chunks, SAR to FinCEN)
- the correlation elevates "noise" into an open case
- the internship dies quietly in the middle of it

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. The outbreak

The refugee camps on the frontier have been growing for months — displaced villagers fleeing bandits and border pressure. They were always undersupplied.

Now cholera is in them.

This is not a failure of Colin's common sense. He set up sanitation where he could reach — the capital, the towns, the army camps. But the frontier is far, the camps are ten times over capacity, and the clean water and latrines he built for the *post-war* population cannot hold against a *refugee flood*.

The disease breaks out in the places his reach never touched, not the places he fixed.

## 2. Why magic cannot scale to it

The locked rules do the work here:

- health potions mend the body — tissue, wounds. They do not kill bacteria and do not fix dehydration
- healers exist, but they are 1–2% of the population, and even the party's healer treats one patient at a time

A healer can save a dozen. The camps hold thousands. Magic is powerful but *scarce*; the crisis is *scale*.

This is the collision that is Colin's whole thesis: **mundane-but-scalable beats mythic-but-scarce.**

## 3. The fix (and why it needs money)

The answer is modern, cheap, and mundane — and it must be bought in bulk:

- **oral rehydration salts (ORS)** — the star. A 50-cent packet of salt + sugar + clean water that drops cholera mortality from ~50% to under 1%. Not even really medicine. Electrolyte powder.
- **antibiotics** — kill the bacteria and the secondary infections
- **water purification** — chlorine tablets, portable filters, to stop it at the source
- **IV saline and kits** — for the severe cases

He needs roughly **$20,000 worth, fast** — days, not the months his weekly gold rhythm was built for.

The key insight: he *can* hand-carry one box of antibiotics (that was the manageable phase's gift). He **cannot** hand-carry ORS + antibiotics + IV for a camp of thousands. The need outgrew what one guy can carry, so he has to *buy in bulk*, and bulk needs money.

## 4. The logistics chain (this is the real trap)

The cholera is solvable. The logistics are not — and every step leaves a trace:

- **buy in bulk** — large purchases, a card, a supplier, a paper trail
- **take delivery at his apartment** — boxes arriving at a student's door, a signature, a receipt
- **lie to his sister** — she already worries; now there are pallets of medical supplies and no explanation that survives a second question
- **teleport the cargo** — by now the kingdom has recovered enough mage infrastructure to semi-consistently run cargo teleports, but heavy mass is expensive and it still requires the mages' cooperation
- **teach them to use it** — ORS ratio, antibiotic dosage, how to chlorinate water; he has to be there, not just drop it
- **verify it is used right** — follow-up, because ORS that is mixed wrong kills as surely as no ORS at all

The gold-selling was discrete — cash, one shop at a time. This is a **visible operation** running through his own front door. That is the difference, and it is why this is the callback that breaks the balance.

## 5. The overdrive

Panic sets in. He skips class, skips an exam, and goes into overdrive — shop after shop, city after city, days in a row, way outside his cadence.

He tells himself he is still careful because he is spreading it across many shops. He does not realize that geographic spread means nothing against a database that has no geography.

## 6. The trip wires (fire near-simultaneously)

**The LeadsOnline cluster:** every transaction logged his real ID, photo, thumbprint. Same person, same face, high-purity unhallmarked gold (his blacksmith fixed the shape, not the metal), $20k+, six to eight shops, three to four days, multiple jurisdictions. That signature — no hallmarks, inconsistent cover stories, deliberate geographic spread — matches organized fencing, not "broke college kid."

**The structuring flag:** he cannot move $20k by hand, so it goes through the bank in chunks over a few days. He tells himself he is being careful. He does not know that *intent to stay under reporting thresholds is itself a federal crime*, independent of the money's origin. The bank's automated systems flag the pattern and file a **SAR to FinCEN**.

**The no-clean-path truth (say it once):** there was no clean way. Cash is traceable, deposits are traceable, suppliers flag large cash too. His real error was not a single slip — it was believing "a little more care" would find a clean path that never existed.

**The dormant note wakes:** the first dealer's memory of the weird bar, sitting unresolved since the Fumble, is now one more loose thread in a file someone is finally assembling.

## 7. The correlation

Independently unremarkable — one flagged SAR among thousands, pawn-database noise — the two data trails get cross-referenced: same name, same week, same anomalous dollar figure.

That is what elevates "huh, weird" into an **open case**, and pulls in attention that ends with the FBI.

The pattern is visible. The walls start to close.

## 8. The internship dies

Quietly, in the middle of all of it.

He stops showing up to the helpdesk. There is no dramatic resignation, no firing. Just a stretch of missed shifts, a "still with us?" email he does not answer, and then the normal life has one fewer thread.

It is not a defeat. It is an erosion — the first concrete thing the double life *took* from him, and he does not even have the bandwidth to notice it go.

## Why This Works

This is the callback where the seductive "I can do both" collapses into visibility:

- the cholera is real and solvable, which is why he cannot refuse
- magic's inability to scale is what forces the modern fix — and the modern fix is what forces the money
- the logistics chain is the real trap, because it turns a secret (gold) into an operation (supplies)
- the overdrive and the trip wires are the mechanical consequence, not a villain
- the quiet death of the internship is the first visible cost of the normal life

## What To Avoid

### 1. Making the disease melodramatic

No. Cholera is grim but the beat is not about the disease — it is about the logistics. Keep the outbreak matter-of-fact; spend the tension on the supply chain.

### 2. A clean, dramatic internship resignation

No firing, no resignation speech. It just stops. Erosion, not decision.

### 3. Colin realizing he is being watched

He does not, yet. The trip wires fire *around* him. The horror is dramatic irony — the reader sees the file forming while Colin thinks he got away with it.

### 4. An instant catch

Nothing happens to him in this beat. The SAR is filed, the case opens, but no one knocks. The catch is a later beat. This beat ends on the *invisible* closing of the walls.

### 5. Making the mage-cargo teleport free

Heavy mass costs real mana and requires the kingdom's cooperation. The teleport is possible now (infrastructure recovered) but expensive — not a bottomless FedEx.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write Callback 2 as the moment the double life stops being clever and starts being visible. The disease is the inciting need, but the logistics are the trap: buying in bulk, taking delivery, lying to his sister, teleporting cargo, teaching and verifying. Let the overdrive break his rhythm, let the trip wires fire around him unseen, and let the internship die quietly in the middle. The scene succeeds when the reader can see the case forming while Colin still thinks he is being careful.
