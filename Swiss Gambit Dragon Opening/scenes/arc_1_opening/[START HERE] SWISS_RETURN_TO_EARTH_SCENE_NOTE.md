# Swiss Return To Earth Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the cold open of Arc 1:

### cold open -> police -> false narrative assigned

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to make the return feel disorienting, grounded, and usable.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- we meet Colin already walking, late night, in an Ohio town, alone
- his clothes are the wrong kind of worn: torn, then mended by hand, still tattered
- he carries no bag, phone, or wallet
- his body keeps bracing for a world that is not here (air with no weight, a flank with no party)
- ordinary people observe him and do not interfere
- he finds a police cruiser and approaches, not afraid, just needing the world confirmed real
- his first line is tired and flat: "What year is it?"
- the cop reads him as a missing person, and the machine starts writing a story for him before he can
- the cult-drugged-and-escaped speculation begins off-screen, assigned by confirmation bias, never claimed by him

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. Opening image

Ohio, roughly 2 to 3 AM. A residential or small-town road, not a city.

Trees, streetlights spaced far apart, nobody around but him.

We meet Colin already moving. He walks with a specific aimless sawtooth: someone navigating a place he should know but cannot place.

The important first impression:

### he does not look like a college kid out late. He looks like a person crossing back into a world that moved on without him.

## 2. The state of him

His clothes are wrong in a specific way.

Not tattered from a fight. Not dirty from the road. Torn, then repaired by hand, with stitching that does not quite match — mended-but-still-tattered.

On him — when the officer finds him — nothing:
- no bag
- no phone
- no wallet

He landed with a small load of kingdom parting-gifts (crystals, a coin, a focus glove, the mirror stone), immediately hid it near the landing site, and walked into town empty-handed. The cop meets a man with nothing on him; the stash is already buried in the trees behind him.

On his body:
- a fading bruise here, an old callus there
- maybe one small scar that reads "sharp thing, healed clean"

He is not malnourished. But there is a dullness to him — a slight vitamin-glow-off, the look of someone who has not eaten modern food or slept a normal bed for two years.

He keeps catching himself:
- bracing for air that has no weight
- half-reaching for something at his flank that is not there
- scanning for exits, for a shield-bearer, for a healer just behind his left shoulder

He is not scared. He is between places.

## 3. The observers

One or two ordinary people see him.

- a night-shift attendant through gas-station glass
- someone taking out the trash and freezing a moment

The point of view can briefly land on Colin from their angle:

A stranger crossing the empty lot, dressed wrong, staring at ordinary things — a vending machine, a streetlight, the glow of a store sign — like he is reading them for the first time.

They do not help.

They watch the way you watch a thing that does not fit.

This gives the "observed" texture without anyone needing to intervene.

## 4. The ask

He finds a police cruiser: parked at a lot, or flagged slow-crawling the empty streets.

He approaches it.

He does not run. He comes over with one hand slightly raised — not in fear, but in that "I need directions I cannot explain" way.

The movement is deliberate, tired, nonthreatening.

## 5. The first line

The officer clocks him instantly:

### kid, wrong place, this hour, probably on something.

But Colin does not act high. He acts like a man genuinely trying to orient himself in a world he already knows. That mismatch is the unsettle.

Then the line, flat and exhausted — not screamed, not frantic, asked almost as a reflex, the way you only ask when the answer is personally meaningful:

### "What year is it?"

The cop stares.

## 6. The false narrative assigns itself

Nothing Colin says is false.

He never claims a story. He does not mention a cult. He does not mention a kidnapping.

But the machine starts writing one anyway, the moment someone has to explain two years of a kid found wandering at 3 AM, clothes hand-mended, disoriented, with no ID.

The officer goes to the radio and calls it in as:

- disoriented
- possibly ill
- uncertain identity
- missing person

The record gets written in his absence, and behind that record, the speculation begins:

### "probably drugged by a cult and escaped."

Nobody knows. It is just the story that fills the gap for people who need one.

The thematic landing of the scene:

### the false narrative is already being written for him, and he has not caught his breath yet.

## 7. Why this works

This opening does the arc's first job before a single callback:

- it shows Colin is displaced, not amnesiac
- it shows his body remembers a world this one is not
- it shows ordinary life observing him rather than absorbing him
- it plants the arc's central pattern in miniature: others define him before he can define himself

And it does all of it without fighting, without exposition, and without the demon-lord war — which stays entirely off-screen. The war exists only as texture in Colin's body-memory, never as a scene.

## What To Avoid

### 1. Starting with the demon-lord war

Dropped entirely. That is the old draft's opening, and it front-loads the least interesting thing. The war is backstory, leaked through body-memory only.

### 2. Making him frantic or theatrical

Tired and flat beats scared. The "what year is it" lands because it is understated, not shouted.

### 3. Malnourishment

He is marked and dull, not starving. Bruises, calluses, a clean-healed scar, a vitamin-off sheen. Distress, not deprivation.

### 4. Him claiming the cult story

He never does. The police and media assign it by confirmation bias. Colin remains technically silent and technically truthful — which is what keeps this from becoming a perjury problem later.

### 5. A helpful witness who intervenes

The observers watch and do nothing. That is the point. They are not bad people; they are the ordinary world refusing to make room.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the return as a quiet collapse of normalcy. Meet him walking, let the observers see a man who does not fit, let him ask the year like it is a load-bearing fact, and let the police record begin constructing a person who is not him before he ever gets the chance to speak for himself. The scene succeeds when the reader feels the disorientation in their own chest — the air that has no weight, the flank that has no one on it — not when anyone explains it.

## Short Version

Colin walks out of nowhere into an Ohio night, hand-mended and alone, asking a cop what year it is. The cop files him as a missing person, and the cult-drugged-and-escaped story assembles itself from the gaps. The war stays unwritten; his body carries it instead.
