# Swiss First Callback Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the fifth movement of Arc 1:

### mirror message -> anchor commitment -> kingdom visit -> the banter -> the souvenir box -> gold temptation made physical

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to show the double life's first successful return — and to plant its cost as an object, not an idea.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- the mirror stone glows with a casual, group message: not urgent, a bandit problem, the border lord keeps asking for the Hero's opinion
- Colin says yes, and "yes" means drawing the anchor for the first time on Earth — the slow, committing inscription under the bed
- the mage reaches through and pulls him; the transit is a collaboration, and Colin cannot go back alone
- kingdom-side, the thick warm air almost undoes him before the work (anti-banditry advice, light, systems-logic)
- the exchange lands the arc's thesis as banter: envy + worry + "you know where the door is"
- he brings Earth souvenirs (Dunkin' box, pen, lighter, paper book, wristwatches, solar calculator, antibiotics)
- the paladin presses a "souvenir" box on him at goodbye; on Earth it turns out full of wealth
- he slams it shut, reopens it, holds the gold carefully, and the temptation becomes physical
- the scene ends on the closed box under the bed, not on false confidence

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. Where he is

Late fall, just before finals. Colin has been back three to four months.

He front-loads. He studies weeks early, keeps a buffer, coasts through year-2 IS work on leftover wartime discipline. It looks effortless; it is the same reflex that once kept a party alive through a war.

This discipline is the buffer the arc will spend down. Callback 1 shows it intact.

## 2. The mirror message

The ritual check. First real message since the mage's "did the transit damage you."

This one is casual, offhand — the mage writing while the whole party chimes in over their shoulder, so the warmth of the group leaks through the words:

> *"Bandit problem. Scattered army turned to raiding the river road. Not urgent. But the border lord keeps asking for the Hero's opinion, so I said I'd ask. You busy?"*

The word that matters is **"not urgent."** It is what lets Colin tell himself the lie: this is a friend asking a favor, not a summoning — I can go, do it, be back in time for a study session I already prepared for.

## 3. The commitment beat: the anchor

He says yes. And "yes" means drawing the anchor, for the first time on Earth.

He drags the bed aside. Kneels. Etches the circle into bare floorboard — deliberately, carefully. Not the panic-exit of the later escape; the slow, committing inscription of someone choosing to make a door.

Each mark is a small promise broken and re-made with his own hand: *I'm done with that place* — and then, no I'm not.

## 4. The transit

He powers the circle with a crystal from the stash. Signals.

The mage reaches through from the kingdom side and pulls him. Colin is NOT the teleporter — not yet. The jump is a collaboration.

He cannot go home alone. That dependency is the quiet heartbeat of the callback: every return is, at bottom, a choice the kingdom and he make together.

## 5. Kingdom-side: the air

He arrives at the known anchor point, and it almost undoes him before anything else happens:

The air is warm and weighted. The mana is thick. His body remembers instantly what Earth refused to give back.

One beat of relief. Then the work.

## 6. The work itself

Anti-banditry, played light. Small, contained, not a battle.

Colin applies systems-logic to a scattering problem: routes, supply depots, patrol rotation, paid riders. He narrates a medieval counter-raid in the same flat register as a logistics spreadsheet, because to him that is what it is.

A weekend's worth of "get the roads working and the riders paid." Not a war.

## 7. The banter (the arc's thesis, spoken)

The exchange is the heart of the scene, and it lands because it is *banter*, not theme. Keep the shape:

The party envies a world with no monsters, but worries that he is nobody there, and they cannot see that they have named the exact thing eating him:

> **Party:** "It's strange. No monsters where you come from. That part, honestly, sounds nice. But — you're really just... nobody there?"
>
> **Colin:** "Yeah. I'm okay being nobody there."
>
> **Party:** "...Alright. I'm glad you're okay being nobody *there*. Because being nobody *here* is miserable. You're not nobody here. So if you're fine out there, good. But if you're not — you know where the door is."

And the gold line, said as a careless afterthought, which is the first real tremor of the whole arc:

> **One of them (the tank, casually):** "Can't you just sell the gold? You have the key to our vault. Borrow a hundred kilos, sell it there — that's a castle's worth, isn't it? Or do you need more?"
>
> **Colin** (laughs it off — from their side it is *nothing*, but part of him files the number away): "I'll... keep that in mind."

## 8. The souvenirs (locked list)

Light, high-value-per-gram, no bulk. He brings:

- a **Dunkin' box** he holds — not sacramental, IMMEDIATELY devoured; the comedy is the speed and the crumbs and "these are fried dough, give me the one with the sprinkles"
- a **ballpoint pen** — the marvel of ink that never needs an inkwell and writes through rain
- a **cheap lighter** — undercut by the party's deadpan "can't you just use fire magic?"; slightly useless, which is the joke
- a **handheld mass-produced paper book** (rope bookmark, rubber-band closure) — the object-as-craft marvel
- **five cheap adjustable wristwatches** (Temu-tier, clasp bands) — one each for the party; hand-wave the timezone as "kingdom day roughly matches Earth's 24/7, close enough he does not over-explain"
- a **solar calculator** — gag gift: it does sums without an abacus, forever
- **antibiotics** — the genuinely world-changing item; it does NOT undercut potions (potions mend wounds, antibiotics cure infection/disease), and it is a small package that fights camp-fever

## 9. The souvenir box (the goodbye)

At goodbye, the paladin presses a box on him — "our souvenir."

Colin assumes a trinket. Thanks them. Packs it.

## 10. Earth-side: opening it

Alone, back in the apartment, he opens the box.

Crystals. Some tools. A couple potions. **And gold and jewelry.**

He slams it shut instantly, like he has seen something forbidden.

Stands there a moment.

Opens it again, slower. Lifts a piece out and holds it carefully.

The inversion lands on the reader: in the kingdom he treats gold like nothing — grain, loose change, numbers in a vault he had the key to. But here, in this dead room, in the world where he is nobody, it weighs what it weighs.

## 11. The close

He does not sell it. Not yet.

But he sits with it for a while. Then the box goes under the bed, with the rest of the stash — and it changes the room.

It is the first thing in his Earth life that is worth something, and he knows it, and that knowledge is the door Callback 2 will walk back through.

The scene ends on the **closed box under the bed**, with the party's parting line still warm in his ears:

### "you know where the door is."

He does.

## Why This Works

This scene plants the arc's real engine without a fight:

- the "not urgent" message makes the first return feel voluntary — and the anchor-drawing makes clear it never really is
- the mage-pull keeps Colin dependent on the kingdom (thematically: he can only return when they cooperate)
- the banter names the wound (nobody there) and comforts it in a way Colin cannot accept
- the souvenir box converts the gold temptation from an idea into an object — tactile, shut, and reopened
- and the close replaces false confidence with something heavier: a closed door he now knows how to open

## What To Avoid

### 1. Making the bandit problem a battle

It is advice and logistics, played light. No war scene.

### 2. A summoning feel

The message is casual, the return is voluntary. The pull toward the kingdom is relational, never compelled.

### 3. "I can do both" as the landing

Cut it. The landing is the box — the temptation made physical, not the reassurance.

### 4. Colin self-teleporting

At Callback 1 the mage pulls him both directions. Colin does not become his own door until later (by Callback 2's end / 3).

### 5. Overexplaining the souvenirs or the timezones

Gifts land by reaction, not lecture. The wristwatch timezone is hand-waved, not explained.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the first callback as a casual favor that turns into a commitment the moment he kneels to draw the anchor. Let the kingdom-side air hit him, let the banter name the wound, let the souvenirs humanize the party, and end on the box: a care package that is also a temptation, shut, reopened, and put under the bed where it quietly changes everything.

## Short Version

A casual "not urgent" message pulls Colin back for a weekend of bandit advice, an anchor he must draw with his own hand, and a mage who has to reach through to bring him. The party feeds him their company, envies and pities him in one breath, presses a souvenir box on him at goodbye — and alone on Earth he opens it to find wealth, slams it shut, and reopens it, because the temptation is now physical, and it is under the bed, and he knows exactly where the door is.
