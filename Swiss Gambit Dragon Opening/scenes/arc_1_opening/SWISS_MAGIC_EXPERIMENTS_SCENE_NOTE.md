# Swiss Magic Experiments Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the fourth movement of Arc 1:

### the forest -> small spells -> the air does not answer -> homesickness made physical

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to let Colin reach for the magic and find that Earth will not give it back the way the kingdom did.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- Colin walks to the forest like a normal kid going for a walk — trying to be ordinary, and failing at it by going somewhere he hopes will feel like home
- he goes alone, having declined the hangout in the last beat
- he is not testing Earth as a scientist; he is checking whether he is still himself
- he casts small, cheap things only — analyze, a little telekinesis, a cheap glow — no teleport (he has no anchor yet)
- the dead-mana air answers wrong: some spells trickle, some do not work at all, and he cannot tell which is the anomaly
- the forest is the closest Earth gets to kingdom terrain, and it still comes up dead — that is the ache made physical
- the half-hope he carried ("maybe Earth is only quiet, maybe my power is dimmed, not gone") dies here

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. Walking there

He does not teleport. He does not ride anything special.

He walks, or takes the ordinary bus, like a normal person going for a walk — which is itself the point: he is *trying* to be someone who belongs here, and the errand keeps revealing he does not.

The forest is chosen because it is the closest Earth gets to kingdom landscape. Quiet. Open. Away from walls and streetlight and other people's eyes.

He is half-hoping that in the open, away from the city, the air will feel closer to the world he left.

## 2. What he casts

Small and cheap only. This is not a demo and not a training montage.

- `analyze`, pointed at nothing in particular, just to feel the motion of it
- one small telekinesis — a leaf, a stone, a coin, lifting a breath
- maybe a cheap glow between his palms, held for a second

The intent is not to test Earth.

The intent is to brush against the language of the place that asked him how he is doing.

## 3. What answers

The air does not answer the way it used to.

- in the kingdom, air had weight and warmth; casting felt like pushing against something alive
- on Earth, there is no push-back and no give — like swinging at water and hitting nothing, or shouting into a room with no walls

Some spells run at a miserable trickle. Some simply will not catch at all, and he cannot yet tell which is the anomaly.

It is the fantasy equivalent of checking his own pulse in a soundproof room.

## 4. The half-hope dies

He carried a private hope into the forest: that Earth was not dead, only *quiet*; that his power was dimmed, not gone; that maybe he had misread it in the early disorientation.

The forest disabuses him.

The one honest realization, felt in the body rather than thought:

### there is no mana here. There never was. Home is a place where the air is not silent, and this is not that place.

## 5. The close

He does not rage. He does not mourn theatrically.

He just stops the small spell, pockets the crystal, and stands in the open air that refuses to mean anything back to him.

Then he walks home — the ordinary walk, again — carrying the new knowledge that part of him works nowhere but the kingdom, and the kingdom is drifting.

## Why This Works

This scene earns the homesickness without a single fight:

- the walk establishes "trying to be normal" failing through action, not narration
- the small spells keep it from becoming a row of tricks
- the dead-mana answer converts an abstract magic rule into a physical, emotional fact
- the forest (Earth's closest stand-in for kingdom terrain) makes the wrongness specific and visual
- and the dead half-hope gives Colin his first hard, private reason to keep glancing at the mirror stone

## What To Avoid

### 1. Teleportation

He has no Earth anchor yet. The teleport experiment is a later, deliberate, anchor-drawing beat — not here. Nothing in this scene should move him magically.

### 2. A row of tricks

Three cheap things, maximum, and each one short. The point is the silence after, not the spectacle.

### 3. Angry or melodramatic grief

He does not scream at the sky. The realization lands quietly. Restraint is the emotion.

### 4. A lecture on mana rules

No one explains anything. The reader feels the dead air through the failed casts, not through exposition.

### 5. A "solution" or comfort

Nobody fixes this. He walks home with it. The forest gives nothing back, on purpose.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the experiment as an errand of longing. Walk him to the forest like a normal kid, let him cast three cheap things that the dead air will not answer, and let the half-hope he carried die without a fight. The scene succeeds when the reader feels the *silence* of Earth's air in their own chest — not when any rule is explained.

## Short Version

Colin walks to the forest to feel the magic again, casts a few cheap spells, and finds that Earth's air does not answer back — some things trickle, some do not work, and the half-hope that his power was only dimmed dies in the open silence. He walks home carrying the knowledge that the part of him that works is in a kingdom that is now drifting.
