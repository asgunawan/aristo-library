# Swiss Baseline Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the third movement of Arc 1:

### baseline settling -> the "not today" refusal -> the mirror stone -> the mage's message -> magic experiments as homesickness

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to feel the double life begin, not as a plot event, but as a daily habit of withdrawal.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- settle Colin into the false-normal rhythm: college, the briefly-famous-returned-person, money as a foreign object, a body that keeps reaching for the war
- the sister is the anchor-line — a text, a shared errand, proof that this life has a handhold
- a friend (or the sister) says "hey, want to hang out?" and Colin says "not today" — a tiny ordinary refusal he does not even notice making
- later he checks the mirror stone — a small ritual he has started, hoping and half-not-expecting anything
- this time it is not silent: the mage has left a message
- the message is the ache — the mage asks about his body, not his feelings, and it is somehow lonelier and warmer for that
- that message is what triggers the magic experiments, not as diagnostics, but as homesickness
- he runs one or two cheap spells to feel like himself again, and the dead-mana air answers wrong

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. Settling, as texture

No one of these is a scene on its own. They are a montage with a pulse.

### College as an alien language

He is year 2 now. He is not lost in the material — he is too sharp at it, because two years of wartime logistics made the coursework feel like a child's puzzle. A professor states something inefficient and Colin's hand itches to restructure it. He catches himself and says nothing.

### The people problem

He is the briefly-famous returned missing person, and it is already curdling. Acquaintances handle him with a soft, cautious gentleness that is worse than hostility — the way you handle someone unstable. He cannot correct it without explaining, so he does not.

### Money as a foreign object

Normal transactions feel invented. Rent, groceries, a textbook. He does not have money, and for two years money did not work like this. He knows the internship search is coming, and the absurdity of the High Chancellor needing a real job sits heavy and unspoken.

### The body will not stop

He scopes rooms for exits. He wakes at kingdom-battle hours. He reaches for a sword that is not there. The war is an accent he cannot shed.

## 2. The anchor-line

Wherever he is, the sister is the reminder that this life has a handhold.

A text. A hallway pass-by. A shared errand.

She is proof of normal. And she is the first person he starts, very slightly, holding at arm's length — not out of coldness, but because part of him is still on the other side.

## 3. The refusal

The beat that names the double life:

- Friend or sister: "Hey, want to hang out?"
- Colin: "Not today."

Tiny. Ordinary. He does not even notice he is doing it.

This is the burnout starting — one small refusal at a time. It is not that Colin is cold. It is that he is not fully present in any of it, and he cannot tell the difference yet.

## 4. The mirror stone ritual

Later, he checks the mirror stone.

This is already becoming a small private ritual: feed it a little mana, watch for the glow. He is hoping and half-not-expecting anything. It has been silent so far, and its silence has been its own kind of weight.

He is a little more eager for this than for the unread texts on his phone. That fact, by itself, is the quiet reveal.

## 5. The mage's message

This time — not silent.

The mage has left a message. The mage, per the locked rules, is the teleporter; Colin is not. So the message is the mage taking responsibility for the transit, worried in the only register they have — technical, precise, quietly tender:

> *"Did the return transit damage you? I calculated it would not, but I could not be certain after the — anyway. Tell me you arrived whole."*

It asks about his body, not his feelings. It is somehow lonelier and warmer for that.

This is the ache, and it is the point of the whole scene.

## 6. The close

Baseline holds him up all the way through, and then the last thing in the beat is a quiet, wrong feeling:

### the kingdom is drifting — and the silence on that side is no longer just distance, but absence.

And then, still holding the mirror, the thought that breaks the rhythm:

### "I should test this out."

The message has reminded him what he left. Now he wants to feel the magic again. Not to test Earth — to check whether he is still himself.

He grabs the bag.

He does not hang out with friends.

He goes to the forest.

That handoff belongs to the next scene. Baseline ends on the decision.

## Why This Works

This scene does the arc's third job:

- it settles the false-normal life into a rhythm
- it plants the burnout as a *habit* (the "not today"), not an event
- it gives the kingdom-side a face (the mage) and a voice (the message)
- it ends on the drift — the first hint that the kingdom's silence is turning into something Colin will not be able to ignore
- and it closes on a decision that pulls him back toward the kingdom-side, alone

The war is still never shown. It reaches through a message.

## What To Avoid

### 1. A played internship/media/reunion scene

Skip. Texture only.

### 2. Making Colin proactively cold or withdrawn

He does not "choose" to refuse the hangout; the "not today" slips out, and he misses it. The refusal is unconscious — that is what makes it tragic rather than mean.

### 3. Running the magic experiments here

Save them. The spells are the next scene's payload. Baseline should stop at the *decision* to go, not the casting.

### 4. Explaining the mirror stone or the mana rules out loud

No lecture. The ritual of checking carries everything.

### 5. Resolving the drift

The scene ends on the drift and the decision. The answer is the next scene's problem.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write baseline as a settling rhythm with one tiny crack: a "not today" that slips out, a mirror checked out of a habit that is already more eager than his phone, and a message from the mage that asks about his body because that is the only warmth they know how to send. Let that message end the scene on a decision — grab the bag, skip the hangout, head for the forest — and stop there, so the spells that follow can land as their own beat.

## Short Version

Colin settles into normal life, says "not today" without noticing, checks the mirror stone out of an already-stronger habit, and finds the mage asking — technically, tenderly, about his body — whether the transit back damaged him. The message reminds him what he left, and the beat ends on a decision: grab the bag, skip the friends, go feel the magic again.
