# Swiss False Reintegration Scene Note

This is a rough scene-direction note for the second movement of Arc 1:

### afterimage of processing -> sister reunion -> performing normalcy -> dead-mana curiosity

The goal is not to lock final prose.

The goal is to land Colin in "false normalcy" while the war keeps reaching through the ordinary.

## Short Answer

The strongest version is:

- skip the police interrogation, the full reunion montage, and the media scrum as played scenes
- show the *afterimage* of processing in fragments (blanket, form, analized by the system)
- land the emotional heart on a single anchor: the sister, two years younger, now at the same college
- compress the reunion into one apartment beat, not a montage of hugs
- the mundane monologue is the balm: her droning about something ordinary proves normal still exists
- Colin performs recovered-ness through tiny domestic pantomime, never by saying "I'm okay"
- his body betrays him: one aborted reach for a sword that is not there
- the scene turns on the dead-mana curiosity: reflex fireball -> a flicker -> "oh right" -> bag -> crystal -> flame roars to life
- end on the crystal in his palm and the room not singing back

That is the cleanest dramatic version.

## Scene Logic

## 1. What we skip, and why

Do not play these straight:

- the interrogation — the record was already written in the return beat; re-showing the cops is redundant
- a hug-montage reunion — too many faces at once, none of them get to matter
- a press conference — the media is ambient noise, not a set piece

The opening's job is to land Colin in normalcy, not to document the re-entry log.

## 2. Afterimage of the system

Fragments, not a scene:

- a foil blanket he did not ask for
- fluorescent light
- a form: name, age, last known address — two years stale
- a nurse noting the hand-stitching on his sleeve
- a social worker's too-kind voice

None of it hostile. All of it *sorting* him.

He answers in tired monosyllables — truthful but hollow. Nothing false. And still, by the time he is released, the file that closes on him is a story *about* him, not *by* him.

## 3. The anchor: the sister

The sister is two years younger than Colin.

Because of the two-year gap, she is now at the *same* college as him. The years he missed let her catch up to him. She did not lose two years; she used them.

That is quietly devastating, and it should sit underneath the scene without being announced.

This is the relationship that hurts most, and we hold other faces (the friend who drifted, the wider family) for later. Spend no extra coin here.

## 4. The apartment beat

The scene proper lands in an apartment — lean toward Colin's (or a shared one), small, ordinary.

The sister is droning about something — a class, a roommate, a grocery run. The mundane monologue is the balm. It is the proof that normal still exists, and for a few seconds he can almost believe it.

Then the exchange that is the scene's spine:

- Colin: "You should search for your first internship, y'know."
- Sister (quick, laughing-painful): "You're missing for two years and the first thing you do is search for a job, lol."

That quip is the whole weight of the scene. It names the gap without softening it, and it lets him off the hook of saying anything heavy at all.

He does not say "I'm okay." That line is cut. Instead he *performs* okay with his hands.

## 5. The tell

The buried wrongness is physical, wordless, brief:

- he reaches for the sword at his belt — and grabs nothing
- he converts the motion: hand drops to the phone in his pocket instead
- he glances at the table and remembers there is no magical lamp to turn off anymore
- he plugs the phone in to charge — the inane domestic ritual
- he turns off the light

Normalcy, assembled out of tiny mechanical moves. Convincing to everyone but the reader, and convincing to his sister exactly long enough.

## 6. The dead-mana bridge

In the dark, alone — after the performance is over — the war walks back in.

Beat for beat:

1. Reflex cast: he lifts a hand and calls a `fireball` the way he always has, toward the empty air — and gets a *flicker*. A match-flame, out before it catches. `analyze` was too cheap to prove anything; this is the real test, and Earth barely answers.
2. "Oh right." — flat, to himself. He remembers where he is.
3. He reaches under the bed and takes out the bag — dug back up from the landing site and now hidden in a compartment, so the room still reads as an ordinary apartment on the surface. Inside: the kingdom parting-gifts, his hold on the kingdom-side he kept from view.
4. Out of it, a crystal. `fireball` again, through the crystal, the hard way — closed in his palm, not flung, so the room stays dark and unmarked.
5. This time it is **large** — a full, hot, living flame cupped in his hand, and for half a second the room lights like kingdom air.

The crystal is a crutch — a stored reservoir he needs precisely because Earth will not feed him. Kingdom air is thick with ambient mana; Earth air is a vacuum, and through the crystal he can feel exactly how empty it is: the same spell that just *went out* is suddenly **white-hot**.

## 7. The close

He closes his fist over the flame and snuffs it, and lets the room *not* sing back.

End on the darkness after.

It is the dead-mana emptiness made tangible, and it is the first quiet proof that "home" no longer runs on anything he understands.

## Why This Works

This scene does the arc's second job without a callback:

- it shows the system already sorting him
- it gives the "what he is trying to protect" a face (the sister)
- it shows the false-normalcy as a performance he assembles with his hands
- it plants the magic-curiosity as a real, tactile beat tied to the dead-mana rule
- and it confirms the sword is still reaching through the ordinary

The war is felt, never shown.

## What To Avoid

### 1. A played interrogation or press conference

Skip it. Afterimage only.

### 2. A hug montage

One sister, one room, one quip. That is the whole reunion.

### 3. "I'm okay"

He never says it. He performs it. The lie is told by hand, not by mouth.

### 4. Making the sister melodramatic

She is droning, then she quips, then she lets it go. The weight is in what she does not force.

### 5. Explaining the dead-mana rule out loud

No lecture. The flickered `fireball`, the "oh right," and the crystal doing the work say everything.

## My Blunt Recommendation

Write the false reintegration as a quiet pantomime. Skip every process scene, land on the sister, let her quip carry the gap, and let Colin's body keep reaching for a sword as his hands do normal things. Then, alone in the dark, let the dead-mana rule arrive the way it should — a reflex that returns silence, an "oh right," and a crystal that finally makes the emptiness feel like something.

## Short Version

Colin is processed without being interrogated, sits with a sister who caught up to his age while he was gone, performs normalcy by plugging in a phone and turning off a lamp, and — alone, in the dark — calls a fireball into dead air, gets only a flicker, says "oh right," and pulls a crystal so the same spell roars to life in his hand.
