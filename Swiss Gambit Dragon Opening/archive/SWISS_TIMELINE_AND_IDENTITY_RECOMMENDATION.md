# Swiss Timeline And Identity Recommendation

This note answers the current cluster of questions together:

- Is 2 years enough?
- How old should he be?
- Should he be a student, programmer, or something else before transfer?
- What is the least-wrong baseline that keeps the story coherent?

## Short Answer

The strongest current version is probably:

- age: 20 when he returns
- pre-transfer: second-year broad practical student
- fantasy-world absence: about 2 years
- Earth-side anchor: light but real, not orphaned and not heavily rooted

That is the cleanest balance.

## Why 2 Years Is Actually Good

I think 2 years is close to the sweet spot.

It is long enough that:
- he can be genuinely transformed
- he can form real bonds in the fantasy kingdom
- he can plausibly take part in a demon-lord-scale crisis
- his old Earth life no longer fits cleanly

But it is short enough that:
- he is still recognizably young
- returning to school or civilian life is not absurd on paper
- the missing-person angle still works
- he has not fully calcified into a fantasy-world adult ruler

That last part matters a lot.

If you make it 4 years or more, then several things start changing:
- Earth life becomes harder to plausibly resume in any form
- the fantasy world starts looking like his obvious primary home
- his political maturity may feel too settled
- the awkwardness of Arc 1 gets weaker because he is less half-formed

So for this specific story, longer is not automatically better.

## Why 16 Is Too Young

I would avoid making him 16.

That creates problems you probably do not want:
- the fantasy-world authority gets harder to buy
- Earth-side legal issues become even more intense
- the story gets pulled toward child-vulnerability dynamics
- political scenes can start feeling distorted by age alone

Also, the specific tone you seem to want is not "kid hero thrown into geopolitics."

It is more like:
- young adult
- underestimated
- not fully formed
- old enough to be taken seriously once he speaks well

That points more toward 19 to 21, not 16.

## Why 20 Works Better Than 18

18 can work, but 20 is probably easier.

At 20, he can still be:
- immature enough to feel strange in diplomacy
- young enough to have a broken student life
- easy to underestimate

But also:
- old enough that fantasy-side leadership does not feel quite as silly
- old enough to plausibly have some independent housing, money issues, and administrative freedom
- old enough that a 2-year transformation feels substantial rather than impossible

So I think 20-on-return is the best target.

That implies roughly:
- transferred around 18
- returns around 20

Which is very usable.

## What He Was Before Transfer

The exact label matters less than the function.

He should be someone who:
- is smart but not specialized to the point of cliché
- knows enough about modern systems to notice what the kingdom lacks
- can look things up and synthesize quickly
- is not already a finished adult professional

That is why broad practical student still seems strongest.

## Best Major Fit

Because you are a programmer and want something you can write comfortably, I would not throw away that familiarity entirely.

The best compromise is probably:

### information systems

Why this is probably the best fit:
- it keeps enough tech familiarity that you can write him naturally
- it is broader and less stereotyped than pure CS genius protagonist
- it supports systems thinking, workflows, data, logistics, and problem translation
- it fits a character who becomes a bridge rather than an inventor-god

If you want a second option, use:

### supply chain / logistics

That is also very strong, but it may be a little less intuitive for you to write from the inside if your natural language is closer to tech.

So my recommendation is:

### an information-systems student with broad practical instincts and some logistics-minded habits.

That gets you most of the benefits without locking him into "hacker chosen one" nonsense.

## Orphan Versus Anchor

I do not think full orphan is the best choice anymore.

It solves a tactical problem, but weakens the emotional shape.

The healthier version is:

### one light but real Earth anchor

For example:
- an aunt who helped raise him
- an older sister he is not constantly close to but does care about
- one parent with a somewhat strained but real relationship

This gives you enough that:
- his disappearance matters
- his return matters
- Earth feels like a place of human ties, not just infrastructure

But not so much that every scene becomes family-hostage realism.

## What The 2 Years Probably Look Like

If you want the cleanest version, the two fantasy years probably should not mean he single-handedly solved an empire from scratch.

Better:

### Year 1
He survives, becomes useful, earns trust, and helps turn a catastrophic losing position into a survivable one.

### Year 2
He becomes politically important because he helped coordinate the kingdom's survival, helped repel the demon lord threat, and became tied to the postwar stabilization problem.

That is more believable than "teen student fully became legendary emperor in 24 months."

He should return as:
- war-tested
- respected
- maybe granted office or authority

But still emotionally unfinished.

That is the sweet spot.

## Why He Still Prefers Earth

Yes, it is very justifiable that he would rather live on Earth.

In fact, that is one of the story's strengths.

He can love the kingdom and still prefer Earth because Earth offers:
- stable medicine
- working infrastructure
- normal entertainment
- privacy
- lower daily stakes
- the right to be ordinary

That is extremely believable.

The fantasy world matters because of bonds and obligation.
Earth matters because it is where life is allowed to be normal.

That is not a contradiction.
That is the whole tension.

## Best Arc 1 Shape

Your instinct here is probably correct too.

Arc 1 should likely be:
- re-adaptation attempt
- double life strain
- some gold-selling and procurement improvisation
- increasing intelligence attention
- repeated fantasy-world pullbacks
- slow realization that normal student life cannot be sustained
- Swiss recognition as the end-of-arc break

That gives the story a clear tonal shift:

### first half
awkward, tense, almost darkly funny double life

### end of arc
he chooses diplomacy and statehood on purpose

That is much stronger than starting with full formal diplomacy immediately.

## My Recommended Baseline

If I had to lock a neutral version right now, I would use:

### He is an 18-year-old information-systems student when summoned, lightly tethered to Earth through one real adult relative and a few ordinary social ties. He returns at 20 after roughly two fantasy years, having helped a kingdom survive and repel a demon-lord-scale crisis, but not having fully grown into the political role he was forced to occupy there.

That version gives you:
- enough transformation
- enough awkwardness
- enough Earth pull
- enough fantasy legitimacy
- enough room for Arc 1 to actually be about him

## Short Version

Keep him around 20 on return, make the absence about 2 years, avoid making him 16 or a fully settled older adult, and use a broad practical student identity such as information systems. That gives you the best balance between Earth awkwardness, fantasy credibility, and the protagonist-centered double-life story you seem to want.
