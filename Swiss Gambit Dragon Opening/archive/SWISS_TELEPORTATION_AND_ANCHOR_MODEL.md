# Swiss Teleportation And Anchor Model

This note cleans up the current Swiss teleport logic around anchors, search, and the Switzerland escape.

## Short Answer

Your basic idea works.

The cleanest version is:

- teleportation needs two anchors
- the anchor is a prepared circle, not the power source itself
- the caster stands at the departure anchor
- the caster sends out a searching magical "eye" or probe along a mentally traced path
- once the probe finds and confirms the destination anchor, the teleport can complete
- for the Ohio escape, the strongest departure anchor is a real emergency circle hidden inside his apartment

That is coherent.

## What Teleportation Should Be

The easiest way to make it feel understandable is:

### teleportation is not opening a permanent portal.

It is more like:

### establishing a temporary locked route between two prepared points.

That is good because it means:

- it can be faster and cheaper than portal creation
- it still needs setup
- it still rewards preparation and saved locations
- it does not make global movement trivial

## What The Anchor Actually Is

The anchor should be:

### a stable magical address marker.

Not a battery.
Not the spell itself.

It is what lets the destination be found, confirmed, and safely locked.

That means the circle can be:
- painted
- etched
- carved
- otherwise laid down in a durable pattern

It does not need to be actively magical all the time.

It only needs to be:
- accurate enough
- intact enough
- recognizable enough to the spell

That part is strong.

## How Fragile Anchors Should Be

The cleanest rule is probably:

### Earth-made painted anchors are moderately fragile.

That means:
- spray paint or floor paint can work
- small damage, smearing, or bad concealment can degrade the jump
- the circle is usable if kept intact enough
- it is not so delicate that one dust particle ruins everything

By contrast:
- etched anchors are more stable
- carved or magically reinforced anchors in the fantasy world can be very robust

This is a good tiering rule.

## The Search-Eye Model

I think your "eye" idea is the right one.

The cleanest phrasing is probably:

### the caster projects a searching probe from the departure anchor toward the approximate destination region, then uses that probe to hunt for the destination anchor and lock onto it.

That gives teleportation three distinct stages.

## Stage 1: Approximation

He needs:
- rough geographic target
- enough directional certainty to send the probe the right way

This can come from:
- maps
- addresses
- coordinates
- landmark knowledge
- prior scouting

Longer distance should make this harder and more expensive.

That makes sense.

## Stage 2: Search

Once the probe reaches the rough area, it begins searching for the matching anchor.

This is where:
- line of sight is not required
- walls do not stop the probe completely
- clutter, distance, and complexity still matter

So the probe is not a camera drone exactly.

It is more like:

### a magical sense-organ being pushed through the world until it finds the right address marker.

That is a good image.

## Stage 3: Lock And Transit

Once the destination anchor is found, the caster locks the route and completes transit.

That means:
- the anchor matters for final precision
- the search is what costs effort
- the actual jump is the payoff after the hard part succeeds

This is a clean rule set.

## Why The Kingdom Relay Is Better

This also explains why the Swiss escape should be:

### Ohio -> Kingdom -> Swiss storage unit.

Why:

- his kingdom location is already a saved or stable point
- the kingdom has abundant mana
- the long-range search toward Switzerland is easier from there than from a dead-mana Earth apartment
- it preserves the sense that Earth is constraining him

So he should not teleport directly from Ohio to Bern unless you want him to look much looser and stronger than the story needs.

The relay version is better.

## What His Ohio Departure Anchor Should Be

Of your current options, I think the strongest choice is:

### a pre-painted emergency circle inside his apartment, concealed under ordinary furniture or a rug when not in use.

Why this is better than the alternatives:

- it means he planned for emergency exit before the final crisis
- it gives the surveillance scene something physical to discover afterward
- it avoids making the last-minute escape depend on drawing a perfect circle while panicking
- it lets the apartment still look mostly normal until the reveal

I would avoid making it fully exposed in the middle of the room all the time.

That is striking, but a little too careless.

I would also avoid making the final escape depend on etching the whole thing that same day unless you specifically want a louder pre-escape anomaly.

The better middle lane is:

### he already had an emergency anchor, but he kept it disguised as part of the room until he had to use it.

That could mean:
- under a rug
- under a movable table
- beneath cheap covering boards
- inside a closet-sized back section he kept private

That gives you drama without making him reckless.

## What The Fiverr Anchor Really Does

The Swiss mural is useful because it gives him:

- a destination marker
- a precise room to lock onto
- a place he can verify through photos, video, or instructions

It should not be treated like the freelancer somehow "built the magic machine."

The freelancer only made:

### the address.

Aiden still provides:
- the search
- the lock
- the transit
- the mana

That division keeps the idea believable.

## How Exact The Setup Should Be

The safest model is:

### address plus images plus destination details are enough for a hard but plausible search-lock.

The livestream can help, but should not be mandatory magic GPS.

Better to treat the feed as:
- confirmation the room is correct
- confirmation the symbol is intact
- confirmation nobody erased it

Not as the entire reason teleportation works.

## What The Watchers Should See

At the Ohio apartment, the U.S. watchers should see:

- Aiden at the departure circle
- chanting or ritual behavior that looks occult or unstable to them
- a sensor-blowing flash or brief visual overload
- vanished subject

That is enough.

They do not need to understand the mechanics.

In fact, it is better if they do not.

## Why This Version Works Dramatically

This model gives you several good story advantages:

- teleportation is powerful, but not casual
- preparation matters
- decoy anchors and false leads can matter later
- storage-unit Switzerland still makes sense
- the Americans can witness something impossible without understanding it

It also helps preserve tone.

Aiden is not strolling through reality effortlessly.
He is forcing a difficult transit using a rule-bound system he understands better than anyone around him.

## My Blunt Recommendation

If I had to lock it now, I would say:

### Teleportation requires a departure anchor and a destination anchor. From the departure point, Aiden projects a magical search-probe toward an approximate destination region, using maps, address details, and prior information to guide it. Once that probe finds and confirms the destination anchor, he locks the route and completes a temporary transit. The Swiss Fiverr mural is therefore not the power source or the spell itself, but the destination address that makes the Switzerland jump possible. For the Arc 1 escape, the strongest version is still Ohio to Kingdom to Swiss storage unit, with U.S. watchers seeing only a flash, a circle, and a vanished subject. His Ohio-side anchor should be a pre-painted concealed emergency circle, and Earth-made painted anchors should be moderately fragile rather than absurdly delicate.

That is coherent enough to build on.

## Short Version

Your anchor-and-search-eye idea works. Treat circles as magical addresses, make the hard part the long-range search and lock, and keep the Swiss escape as a kingdom-assisted relay rather than a clean direct jump from Ohio.
