# Inconsequence Difficulty Models Note

This note captures the latest answer state, which is less "pick a number" and more "what kind of people can realistically do this before the field matures?"

That is the correct framing.

The timescale question is not really about months.

It is about:
- who has enough time
- who has enough body control
- who has enough obsession
- who can survive the sleep bottleneck
- how much the state can accelerate compared to the public

## The Core Problem

You are correctly noticing that different timescales imply different societies.

### If self-awakening is too fast

Then:
- government programs scale too quickly
- serious amateurs start succeeding too often
- Arthur stops mattering too early
- the training culture becomes less frustrating and more straightforward than you want

### If self-awakening is too slow

Then:
- Arthur remains too exclusive for too long
- public culture becomes mostly fake noise
- the state fast lane matters so much that the story risks collapsing into official pipeline scenes
- founder-teacher Arthur has less room to become socially important outside institutions

So the right answer is not a pure middle number.

It is a stratified difficulty model.

## The Best Way To Think About It

The process should probably not be equally hard for all serious people.

That would flatten the story.

Instead, the field should separate people by three things:
- free time and life structure
- bodily / respiratory / concentration suitability
- willingness to live stupidly for long enough

That gives you a more believable early culture.

## A Better Social Model Than "6 To 12 Months For Everyone"

The cleanest answer is probably this:

### For normal employed adults

Without Arthur and without official tools, the path is practically non-viable.

Not metaphysically impossible.
Just socially unrealistic.

They can:
- follow the notes
- get interested
- maybe feel tiny ambiguous things
- maybe keep trying for months

But most will not reach a real proto-core while living a normal life.

This is good, because it preserves the idea that the field is initially hostile to ordinary schedules.

### For high-discipline outliers with lots of time

This includes:
- obsessive hobbyists
- college students with unhealthy flexibility
- athletes in off-season or recovery windows
- underemployed or jobless obsessives
- institution-supported candidates

These people are your plausible early off-pipeline edge cases.

For them, proto-core formation without Arthur should be:
- still rare
- still ugly
- but no longer absurdly impossible

This is probably where the 6 to 12 month instinct belongs.

### For official fast-lane candidates

These people get:
- Arthur's transfer
- monitoring
- clinical sleep support
- data review
- coaching
- selection pressure

Now the process becomes dramatically faster.

Not easy.
Just less blind.

This preserves both truths:
- the state really does have a faster path
- Arthur is still not handing out finished magic

## My Recommended Difficulty Model

If I were locking the system for coherence, I would use something like this:

### Unassisted, ordinary life

Effectively non-viable in the first generation.

Most people:
- fail
- quit
- misinterpret bodily sensations
- never reach anything worth measuring

### Unassisted, rare obsessive outlier

Possible, but ugly.

Best-case outlier:
- may reach a fragile proto-core in roughly 6 to 18 months
- still very likely to lose it repeatedly
- still probably fails the sleep bottleneck many times

This feels right because it avoids both mass replication and total Arthur monopoly.

### Arthur-assisted, early official program

Ignition or proto-core formation can happen much faster.

Rough shape:
- sensation in days
- meaningful catch attempts in days to weeks
- proto-core ignition in weeks for good candidates
- actual overnight survival remains the real engineering problem

This matches the current draft much better than trying to make unaided people too successful too early.

## Why 3 To 6 Months Is Too Fast Right Now

Your gut discomfort here is sound.

If unaided 3-to-6-month success is common among rare but reachable candidates, then:
- state programs accelerate very hard very fast
- athlete and military recruitment pipelines become too efficient too soon
- the field starts looking like a training optimization problem more than a miracle-to-discipline transition

That may be interesting later.
It is probably too early for first-generation rollout.

So I would avoid locking unaided best-case progress that fast unless you want the whole story to move harder into strategic emergence.

## Why 1 To 3 Years Is Too Slow As A Dominant Feel

That range is not impossible.
But if it becomes the dominant perception, then:
- public imitation feels mostly fake
- the training culture explosion gets delayed too much
- Arthur remains too singular
- the official pipeline becomes almost the only real story

That weakens the specific thing you said you wanted.

So I would not center that as the main vibe either.

## The Sweet Spot

The sweet spot is probably:

### Publicly
Most people fail or only get ambiguous edge cases.

### Socially
A few obsessive weirdos make real but tiny progress from public notes alone.

### Institutionally
Arthur plus tools plus selection produce much faster and more credible results.

That means the public story is:
- frustrating
- noisy
- full of bad imitators
- occasionally interrupted by a real freak success

While the official story is:
- faster
- better measured
- more controlled
- still not easy

That is a very strong contrast.

## What This Means For The First Big Break

You were right that receiver training alone does not truly break Arthur's monopoly.

It improves the efficiency of his bottleneck, but does not replace it.

So the first real monopoly reduction probably needs to be one of these:

### Option 1: Sleep-survival protocol

Arthur still ignites people, but now they no longer lose everything so easily.

This reduces his bottleneck by increasing retention.

Strong because:
- consistent with your lore
- keeps technology central
- makes the state fast lane feel valuable

Weakness:
- Arthur is still the igniter for a while

### Option 2: Second igniter

Someone Arthur trained eventually becomes able to perform real ignition on others.

This is the first true human scaling break.

Strong because:
- dramatically changes the political landscape
- proves the founder can create successors
- makes the field feel alive

Weakness:
- if it happens too early, Arthur's uniqueness collapses too fast

## My Recommendation On Sequence

You probably want both, but in order.

### First
Sleep-survival protocol reduces waste and proves the field can preserve fragile success.

### Later
A second igniter emerges and truly breaks Arthur's monopoly.

That sequence is healthier because it prevents the story from skipping too fast from one founder to many founders.

It also makes the second igniter feel earned rather than convenient.

## Best Current Working Answer

If you want one sentence to write with right now, I would use this:

Without Arthur, only rare obsessive outliers with unusual time, discipline, and suitability can hope to reach a fragile proto-core from public notes alone, usually over many months and with repeated failure; the real scale break comes first from sleep-survival technology and only later from a second human igniter.

That feels closest to what your instincts are already circling.

## Short Version

Do not think of self-awakening as one global timescale. Think of it as socially stratified difficulty. For normal people it is near-nonviable at first, for obsessive outliers it is a brutal many-month process, and for the official Arthur-backed fast lane it becomes dramatically faster but still sleep-fragile. That preserves both Arthur's early importance and the later training-culture explosion.
