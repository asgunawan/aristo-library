# VRAINS - Team Cerberus

## Deck and Economy Notes

## Why VRAINS feels different
VRAINS is a data economy.

That means the real value is not just the cards themselves, but:
- account access
- deck completeness
- codes and programs
- data shards and bounty payouts
- one working machine versus three people who need to use it

The triplets begin in the worst possible version of scarcity: not only are they poor, they are poor in a world where cards are code and missing one program can make an entire deck nonfunctional.

## The shared-access problem
The early era only works if the triplets cannot all operate freely at once.

Best source-grounded reading:
- they are stuck with one good headset / one good account / one real access route
- this forces them to choose who gets to duel
- deck choice becomes an economic argument, not just a strategic one

## The deck trinity

### 1. The Hoarder - Thunder / Chaos pile
Function:
- high-yard, high-value, long-resource deck

Core source logic:
- Thunder Dragon roots
- later Colossus / Titan type payoff
- 60-card or Grass-adjacent appetite remains part of the fantasy
- Fairy Tail - Snow and graveyard mass are part of the source's peak-power imagination

Broken-knee starting point:
- strong theme, weak finish
- has volume before it has full conversion

Economic meaning:
- the Hoarder is the sibling who turns junk data into future inevitability

### 2. The Griefer - SPYRAL
Function:
- information abuse, control, humiliation, long combo turns

Core source logic:
- the timeline moved away from full Prank-Kids because the key support arrived too late
- SPYRAL fits the era, the cheater vibe, and the team's information advantage much better
- the deck becomes monstrous once the right core pieces exist

Broken-knee starting point:
- without the real engine piece, it is just annoying agents and failed setup
- becomes fully malignant only once the combo spine is online

Economic meaning:
- this is the deck that best expresses using knowledge itself as a weapon

### 3. The Architect - Orcust
Function:
- machine recursion, loop control, graveyard architecture

Core source logic:
- the most system-oriented deck in the trio
- thrives once its loop pieces are complete
- plays the long game and stabilizes the team's overall output

Broken-knee starting point:
- no proper recycler / no proper loop = all engine, no machine
- once unlocked, it becomes the team's most reliable board-conversion shell

Economic meaning:
- the Architect turns scattered code into infrastructure

## Cerberus Shift economy
The team's special advantage is not simply "we own three decks."
It is that they can choose which deck actually enters the match.

That creates a new economic layer:
- matchup knowledge is capital
- surprise is capital
- forcing the opponent to prepare for the wrong sibling is capital

In this era, identity itself becomes a resource.

## Progression logic

### Stage 1 - Trash decks with missing organs
- all three decks exist in embryo
- none are fully online
- they are living on the fantasy of what the decks could become

### Stage 2 - First key unlocks
- one critical card or program at a time fixes each deck's broken knee
- the team's internal arguments over what to unlock first are part of the economy

### Stage 3 - Bounty viability
- once one sibling becomes stable enough to win reliably, the team can farm the money / data needed to finish the other two
- one complete deck subsidizes the others

### Stage 4 - Full team threat
- once all three decks are online, Team Cerberus stops being three poor players and becomes a flexible raid-boss structure

## Key economic truth of VRAINS
The team does not rise because they are naturally the best.
They rise because they make three incomplete, underfunded identities function as one optimized organism.

That is the real VRAINS fantasy here: not a king, not a commander, but a multi-headed machine that turns scarcity into specialization.
