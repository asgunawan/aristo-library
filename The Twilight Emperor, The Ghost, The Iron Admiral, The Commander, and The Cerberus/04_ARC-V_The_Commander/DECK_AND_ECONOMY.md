# Arc-V - The Commander

## Deck and Economy Notes

## Why Arc-V feels different
Arc-V is a marketized education economy first and a dueling story second.

Power flows through:
- tuition
- projector quality
- parent confidence
- scholarship poaching
- deck access
- who controls the new summoning technologies before everyone else

That means the deck is never just a deck. It is:
- a curriculum
- a recruitment tool
- a marketing signal
- a payroll problem
- eventually a logistics platform for militias and occupied districts

## Baseline economy of the dojo

### 1. Victory Star collapse
- the dojo is already dying when the Commander really takes over
- Tanaka is buried under mortgage / projector debt
- losing a few students means failing tuition intake
- LDS can kill a rival school just by poaching enough talent and humiliating the rest in public

### 2. Why the Commander cannot just buy a better deck
- he is underpaid
- the school is cash-poor
- the useful cards are not just expensive, they are gated through institutional supply
- every major upgrade is tied to either debt, corruption, theft, or a tactical bet on future winnings

### 3. Nezumi's function
Nezumi is not side flavor. He is the procurement hinge of the entire era.

His role in the source:
- LDS-adjacent corrupt manager / supply-chain rat
- controls or leaks access to failed, defective, or gated Pendulum material
- uses debt as a collar
- sees the Commander as both customer and future asset

The important logic:
- the Commander does not "discover" Metalfoes in a noble way
- he mortgages the school's future and buys into forbidden trash through a corrupt middleman
- later phases keep escalating because Nezumi always controls the next tier of weapons unless the Commander can pay, impress, or exploit him

## The deck tech tree

The Arc-V era has a clearer named evolution than I captured before. The story works best if this deck ladder is treated as a military armory.

### Phase 1 - Iron Pike
Formerly the early Iron Will / Iron Statue phase.

Role:
- pre-Pendulum anti-fun survival deck
- proves the philosophy before the real weapons arrive

Economy:
- dirt-cheap
- bargain-bin normal monsters and stun traps
- probably bought by weight, scavenged, or assembled from whatever the collapsing school already had

Core cards from the source:
- 3x Gene-Warped Warwolf
- 3x Alexandrite Dragon
- 3x Mystery Shell Dragon
- Rescue Rabbit as one premium tech piece
- 3x Heat Wave
- 3x Tyrant's Throes
- 3x Faustian Bargain
- Skill Drain if available

How it plays:
- lock effect monsters out
- force the duel onto vanilla-stat math
- punish flashy, effect-heavy students who assume they will be allowed to play normally

Why it matters:
- this is the ideology phase
- the Commander proves you can beat richer kids with discipline, refusal, and field compression

### Phase 2 - Iron Pike & Shot
Early hybrid phase after access to incomplete Metalfoes material.

Role:
- the first bridge between the anti-meta philosophy and Pendulum-era resource conversion

Economy:
- old stun core + defective / incomplete Metalfoes package
- built under debt pressure
- supplied through Nezumi's corrupt line or the equivalent black-market deal

Core cards from the source:
- 3x Gene-Warped Warwolf still as frontline beater
- 2x Tyrant's Throes still relevant
- 3x Metalfoes Steelen
- 3x Metalfoes Goldriver
- 3x Metalfoes Counter
- possibly Skill Drain / similar anti-fun holdovers

How it plays:
- still holds the line like a pike wall
- but now uses Metalfoes scales to pop your own resources and set Counter
- destruction becomes ammunition instead of loss
- deck is clunky, incomplete, and not yet elegant

Why it matters:
- this is the debt-and-experiment phase
- the school is literally field-testing black-market doctrine on children

### Phase 3 - Metal Line Infantry
The first truly coherent Metalfoes army deck.

Role:
- full-power school doctrine
- this is where the dojo becomes Metal Will / Metal Force in practical terms

Economy:
- paid for through entry fees, recruitment fees, and whatever ugly bargain made the fusion package possible
- still cheap by elite standards, but now expensive enough that every student deck is a real investment

Core cards from the source:
- 3x Goldriver
- 3x Silverd
- 3x Steelen
- 3x Volflame
- 3x Rescue Rabbit
- 1x Metalfoes Fusion
- 3x Metalfoes Counter
- 2x Metalfoes Combination
- Fullmetalfoes Alkahest
- Metalfoes Mithrilium
- sometimes Orichalc depending on build stage

How it plays:
- scale up
- pendulum summon several bodies at once
- pop your own cards to set engine pieces
- fuse into Alkahest to steal
- fuse into Mithrilium to bounce instead of destroy
- recycle Metalfoes Fusion forever

Why it matters:
- this is where the deck stops being a deck and becomes a drill manual
- any student who follows orders correctly can execute the line
- this is the foundation of the Metal Will phalanx

### Phase 4 - Metal Force / Dracoslayer Division
Later campaign-grade build, especially once the Arc-V material widens into Synchro Dimension conflict.

Role:
- expeditionary and command-tier build
- no longer just for dojo survival, but for territorial dominance

Economy:
- this is no longer funded by tuition alone
- it depends on supply monopoly, extortion, syndicate ties, campaign loot, or higher-level backing
- Nezumi and later larger institutional toleration still matter heavily

Core cards from the source:
- 12x Metalfoes core infantry
- 3x Luster Pendulum, the Dracoslayer
- 3x Master Pendulum, the Dracoslayer
- 3x Rescue Rabbit
- optional tech like Gofu depending on exact stage
- Metalfoes Fusion
- Metalfoes Counter
- Metalfoes Combination
- Ignister Prominence as the ace
- Mithrilium and Alkahest retained
- later anti-summon war tools like Dimensional Barrier / Solemn-style pressure appear in the broader concept space

How it plays:
- keeps the Metalfoes engine for recursion and scale pressure
- adds Tuners and better command-grade threats
- upgrades the force from line infantry into mobile officer corps / warlord deck
- gives trusted leaders the ability to outclass local Synchro or Action strategies completely

Why it matters:
- this is where the school's curriculum becomes an army tech tree
- the Commander is no longer training a class; he is controlling arms distribution

## Rank-gated distribution

One of the most important things I missed before is that the deck is not distributed equally.

The source strongly suggests a hierarchy like this:

### Rank 1 - Infantry
- pure Metalfoes / line-infantry style lists
- enough to hold checkpoints, defend warehouses, and outplay untrained duelists

### Rank 2 - Officers
- upgraded hybrids with better Pendulum control, extra engines, or privileged cards
- trusted with raids, local command, and zone control

### Rank 3 - Warlords / sector leaders
- Dracoslayer-enhanced or otherwise premium variants
- trusted with the real ace-level weapons
- can function independently without the Commander standing behind them

This matters because the Commander's real power is not only in playing the deck. It is in deciding who gets which version of the deck.

## Colonial economy / insurgency logistics

By the later Arc-V phase, the school economy mutates into war economy.

That means:
- food becomes payroll
- cards become rifles
- better cards become officer commissions
- entry fees become military procurement
- bounties become recruitment incentives
- deck knowledge becomes territorial law

This is why the phrase "children of the metal" works. The students are not just duelists anymore. They are a supply chain with uniforms.

## Key truth of Arc-V

Arc-V is strongest when the deck ladder reads like military modernization:
- Iron Pike = hold the line with cheap bodies
- Pike & Shot = mix old doctrine with dangerous new tools
- Line Infantry = repeatable formation warfare
- Dracoslayer Division = officer-grade mechanized escalation

The Commander is scary because he understands that once you control supply, doctrine, and promotion, every child with a deck becomes part of your army.
