# Zexal - The Iron Admiral

## Deck and Economy Notes

## Why Zexal feels different
Zexal is not primarily a starvation economy like 5D's and not a school-caste economy like GX.

It is a possession economy layered over a cleaner consumer world.

That means the real value is tied to:
- Numbers
- hardware access like D-Gazers and Duel Disks
- local labor and repair capacity
- whether you can turn one impossible asset into an entire working life

## The family economy first

This era works best when the family / workshop situation is concrete.

Current source-grounded picture:
- the protagonist lives near or above the family workshop in the industrial district
- the household is poor enough that extra money matters immediately
- the father is overworked and initially frustrated that his child is more invested in cards than labor
- early card money goes not into luxury, but into bills, groceries, sleeves, repairs, and making the workshop a little less desperate

That is the emotional base layer of the era. The deck is important because it changes the household, not just the duel outcomes.

## The D-Gazer and local capital

The D-Gazer is important because it is basic access hardware, not just an accessory.

Source logic supports:
- old, cracked, used model
- not top-end clean-city tech
- something repaired, inherited, scavenged, or patched together

This matters because Zexal's world is AR-dependent. If the protagonist cannot maintain his own gear, he falls out of the game socially and competitively.

## The Number as first capital

Blackship of Corn is the protagonist's first true impossible asset.

Economically, that means:
- it is not just a card, it is leverage
- it creates the chance to take rarer cards, win high-value duels, and establish a reputation
- it also creates psychological cost, because selling the wrong cards to keep the lights on is part of what makes the curse dynamic hurt

## Deck ladder

### 1. Rank 4 Junk / Lost-and-Found shell

Function:
- the earliest shell exists only to put two Level 4 monsters on board and summon Blackship reliably

Representative low-end tools visible in the source:
- Tin Goldfish
- Kagetokage
- Goblindbergh
- cheap Level 4 extenders

Important note:
- this phase is not truly strong on its own
- it is one Number dragging a bad economic life forward

### 2. The roach package / Maxx "C" windfall

This matters more than I gave it credit for before.

Source logic repeatedly emphasizes:
- Maxx "C" exists in this era
- in-world, people undervalue it because it looks like ugly bug trash
- the protagonist, because he is poor and pragmatic, is willing to buy or grab things others ignore

Why it matters:
- Maxx "C" is one of the biggest power spikes in the era
- it feeds the hand, which feeds both Blackship setups and later machine discard engines
- it is thematically perfect: poor kid, industrial district, bug card no one respects, secretly one of the strongest pieces in the format

This should be treated as a real turning point in the deck economy.

### 3. Early machine stabilization

Before full Iron Fleet, the deck starts looking more like the protagonist's real life.

Representative direction:
- Gadgets
- machine extenders
- Blackship as flagship
- Maxx "C" as hidden engine card

Meaning:
- deck stops being random found-object junk
- starts becoming an actual working-class machine deck

### 4. Machina phase

This is the first big identity lock.

Source-grounded acquisition logic:
- the father finds or brings home cards from scrap or stripped hardware
- Machina cards feel believable because they look like industrial leftovers and structure-deck fodder, not untouchable protagonist ace cards

Representative cards:
- Machina Fortress
- Machina Gearframe
- support machine discard shell
- Maxx "C" still doing hand-economy work

Why it matters:
- Fortress is the first card that makes the deck feel physically heavy
- this is where the workshop and the deck stop being parallel and start being the same story

### 5. Geargia Machina / Iron Fleet

This is the true Iron Admiral deck.

Representative cards / structure from the source:
- Geargiarmor
- Geargiaccelerator
- Geargiarsenal
- Machina Fortress
- Machina Gearframe
- Gear Gigant X
- Blackship of Corn
- Dimensional Prison as important anti-Number / anti-anime trap logic

Why it matters:
- Geargia gives the fleet structure, searching, and discipline
- Machina gives mass and working-class body
- Blackship gives curse, identity, and removal authority

This is the point where the deck stops feeling like "good machine cards" and starts feeling like a navy.

### 6. WDC and late expansion

At WDC stage, the deck is no longer just a local survival engine.

Economic effect:
- duel-disk repair business grows because of tournament traffic
- the family workshop stabilizes or even starts booming
- better sleeves, better tools, better D-Gazers, and better food become part of the protagonist's normal life

Late expansion cards in the source include things like:
- higher-end fleet upgrades
- stronger Extra Deck ships
- city-level prestige pieces

## The workshop -> deck mirror

This is the strongest organizing principle of the era and should stay explicit:

- the family workshop is damaged but functioning
- the protagonist learns repair, tuning, and part-salvage
- his deck evolves from junk to systems
- the better the workshop does, the cleaner and more disciplined the fleet becomes

## Key economic truth of Zexal

The era works best when the player's rise is inseparable from two things:
- the household no longer being crushed
- Blackship of Corn no longer screaming every time he has to sell part of himself to survive

That is why the Iron Admiral arc is good. It turns deck upgrades into class mobility, family relief, and emotional management all at once.
