# Zexal - The Iron Admiral

## Character Notes

### The Protagonist / The Iron Admiral
Role:
- blue-collar Number hunter / machine duelist / mechanic's son

Core traits:
- practical
- obsessive in a contained way
- materially aware
- less theatrical than most Zexal duelists
- uses work ethic and systems-thinking to compensate for lack of chosen-one status

Narrative function:
- grounds Zexal's AR spectacle in workshop labor and family economics
- turns Number hunting into something closer to a profession than a prophecy
- represents controlled obsession rather than friendship-driven heroism

Important life pressure:
- he is still a child trying to help hold a household together
- he is not fully free to indulge the Number because the rent, groceries, repairs, and his father's exhaustion keep dragging him back into reality

### Number 50: Blackship of Corn
Role:
- ace card, curse, emotional anchor, and identity-shaping force

Core traits / narrative behavior:
- associated with hoarding, harvesting, ownership, and removal
- pressures the protagonist's choices rather than simply obeying them
- reacts badly when he sells away the wrong kind of "cargo"
- calms when the deck is treated like a crew rather than inventory

Narrative function:
- gives the era a real emotional center
- makes the dueling identity feel embodied rather than just strategic
- ties economy, deck choice, and psychology together

### Father / Workshop Figure
Role:
- livelihood anchor / practical support / class identity source

Current best reading from source:
- he begins as overworked, skeptical, and worried that the protagonist is wasting time chasing cards instead of helping enough
- he is not romantically validating the obsession at first; he tolerates it because money keeps appearing and bills still have to be paid
- once the protagonist begins handing over meaningful cash, helping stabilize the shop, and easing pressure, the relationship softens
- he still worries about gangs, trouble, and what the card-hunting is doing to his son, but he also becomes proud of the technical competence and the way the protagonist can keep the business alive

Narrative function:
- makes the era distinctly blue-collar
- justifies the Disk Doctor / machine-control path
- prevents the story from becoming purely abstract Number hunting
- turns money into something emotional: not just deck progression, but relief in the household

### Yuma Tsukumo
Role:
- protagonist comparison point

Narrative function:
- represents the brighter, destiny-driven version of Zexal
- the Iron Admiral gains meaning by being the opposite kind of important person in the same world

### Kite / Arclight / Vetrix / Quattro / other Number-space elites
Role:
- ladder markers and world-scale threats

Narrative function:
- show the difference between local predation, elite collecting, and mythic Number conflict
- Quattro is one of the first major public reputation tests
- Vetrix appears to function as one of the clearest upper ceilings of the era
