T1 Ace vs 1939 Fighter Notes

Rules anchor from PSYCHICS.md / NEW_TECHNOLOGY.md / SPANISH_WAR_LESSONS.md:
- T1 top speed about 200 mph.
- Snap is line-of-sight, 100 yd, 6-7 high-cost uses before Soot risk.
- Flick is accurate to about 200-300 m, 2-3 simultaneous vectors, bullet-velocity bearings.
- Barrier stops rifle-caliber fire, but heavy AA / .50 cal / 20 mm can break it.
- 1939 Spitfire Mk I is rifle-caliber armed in-setting logic; later cannon fighters are the real aircraft threat.
- Defense against Aces is jammer + AA + another psychic, not fighter-vs-fighter dogfighting.

Working conclusion:
- In a clean 1v1 visual-range engagement, a ramped and alert T1 should usually beat a 1939 rifle-armed fighter such as a Spitfire Mk I.
- The T1 should not automatically dominate the whole air war. Fighters still control patrol range, altitude, disengagement, escort, spotting, and vectoring heavier counters.
- So: T1 wins the duel more often; integrated air defense wins the campaign more often.

Why the T1 wins the duel:
- Plane cannot easily survive inside Flick range if the Ace gets a firing solution.
- Plane cannot match hover, reversal, or zero-radius vector changes.
- Snap makes one pass or one lining-up solution unreliable.
- Rifle-caliber fire is below the barrier-kill threshold already established in the lore.

Why the plane still matters:
- It is much faster in straight line flight and can refuse the fight.
- It can keep altitude and force the Ace to spend energy climbing or waiting.
- It can spot, shadow, and vector AA, jammers, or another Ace.
- It can attack during the Ace's idle / ramp window, on the ground, or during transit.

Best setting balance statement:
- A 1939 fighter is not an Ace-killer; it is an Ace-finder, Ace-herder, and Ace-delayer.
- Cannon-armed or heavy-MG fighters are the first planes that become true Ace-killers.

If the duel feels too one-sided, add friction here rather than removing T1 dominance:
- Flick accuracy against crossing aircraft is excellent but not trivial; usually kills in 1-2 passes, not instantly at first sight.
- Snap is evasive and positional, not a chase button; 100 yd matters in a merge, not across open sky.
- 360 barrier coverage is expensive, so multiple slashing attacks can wear an Ace down.
- High altitude, cloud, glare, and sun-angle degrade mapping and line-of-sight more for the Ace than for the fighter.
- An Ace below full ramp is in real danger even from rifle-caliber saturation if caught wrong.

Clean verdict:
- Spitfire Mk I vs ready T1 Ace: Ace favored strongly.
- Generic 1939 fighter: depends on armament; rifle-caliber fighters lose, cannon fighters become dangerous.
- Air network vs T1 Ace: network is the real answer, not lone fighters.
