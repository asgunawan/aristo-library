# Doctrine Evolution — How the War Learned to Fight (1936–1944)

The war is not a sequence of battles; it is a **dialectic**. Every doctrine in this file is an answer to the last one — a move, then a counter, then a counter to the counter. The opening assumption was "a psychic is unblockable," and by the end the only winning move was to make psychic power itself impossible. Between those two points, warfare rewrote itself eight times.

Each turn has the same four beats: what they **assumed**, what **awakening** broke it, how they **responded**, and what the response **cost**. The cost matters as much as the response — every doctrine is a trade, and the story is told in what each side gave up.

---

## Turn 0 — The offensive assumption (1936–39): "A psychic is unblockable."

1. **Assumption.** The world fights a WW1-style war — mass armies, trenches, artillery, the line. Onto this, the psychic is bolted as an elite super-weapon. The core belief, confirmed by Spain, is that a psychic cannot be stopped: you can't shoot a teleport, and you can't hold a line against a man who appears behind it.
2. **The German breakthrough doctrine.** An Ace is used to **break breakpoints** — the anti-friction scalpel at the Schwerpunkt. One Ace Snaps in, kills the gun or the commander, Snaps out; the one obstacle that would have stalled the advance is deleted, and the panzers roll. The Ace operates **alone or with air escort only** — aircraft are the only thing fast enough to share a breakthrough with a 200-mph, teleporting man; tanks and infantry are too slow. The tempo is **two-speed**: the Ace breaks the line in minutes, and the ground forces arrive hours later to exploit what's left.
3. **Awakening (none yet).** This is the doctrine *before* it meets a counter. It works flawlessly in the Netherlands, where there is no wall.
4. **Cost — the seed of everything wrong.** Germany is doctrinally **one-dimensional**: it has never defended against a psychic, so it has no defensive doctrine at all. Its whole theory is "the weapon is unstoppable," and its ideology needs that to be true. And the two-speed tempo means the Ace is always *ahead* of the army that must hold what it breaks — a raider, not an occupier.

---

## Turn 1 — Denial, built in advance (1937–39): "Noise is cheaper than signal."

1. **Awakening.** Spain showed France and Britain the same thing it showed Germany — but they read it differently. Germany concluded "the weapon is invincible"; the Allies concluded "we cannot out-psychic Germany, so we must find a way to make the psychic's power *not work*."
2. **Response.** The jammer. It is far easier to *foul* a brain's frequency than to make one work perfectly — noise is cheaper than signal. France builds the **Static Maginot** (the wall); Britain builds the jammer plus **Wooderson** (the Radar-Sync, the sentinel on top of the wall). The answer is not "fight the psychic" — it is *denial*.
3. **Cost.** The jammer is a **wall, not a weapon.** It is indiscriminate (your own psychics can't operate inside it), stationary, fragile, and power-hungry to the point of national commitment. France sacrifices its entire grid and any offensive psychic program; Britain ties its whole defense to one dying man. Both can defend forever and attack never.

---

## Turn 2 — The wall's limits, learned in blood (March–Dec 1939): "A wall isn't a shield."

1. **Awakening.** The war starts over the Netherlands. The Netherlands falls in eleven days — there is no wall there, and the Ace breakthrough works exactly as advertised. Then the Ace rush on London **Shunts** against the jammer and Wooderson: the Snap is denied, and the Aces materialize short, into AA fire and the sea. Germany learns the wall is real — and that it is a *denial zone*, not a shield. It blocks Snap and low flight; it does **not** block high flight, Flicking in from outside the radius, or bombing.
2. **Response.** Germany pivots to **air superiority and vertical attack**: fly *above* the field, bomb the towers and power plants, snipe from beyond the noise radius. From "Snap in and win" to **"siege the island."** The early Battle of Britain is planes and Aces against jammer and sentinel.
3. **Cost.** Germany can no longer conquer Britain by Snap; it must strangle it from outside — a siege, not a conquest. And every sortie burns the thorium clock.

---

## Turn 3 — The T0 problem (1940): "You can't out-punch a god, so starve him."

1. **Awakening.** Wooderson is a strategic weapon, not a soldier. Germany's Aces cannot contest a T0 directly — a T0 dominates a T1. And psychic-against-psychic combat is revealed as **attritional**: both sides burn scarce, irreplaceable psychics, and neither can afford the exchange. The age of the Ace as an unstoppable spear is over; every psychic engagement is now an expensive gamble.
2. **Response.** Don't fight the sentinel — **attack what sustains him.** The war moves sideways into the *resource* war: the mica convoys through the Mediterranean, Scandinavia's neutral trading floor, Brazil's mines. The center holds (wall + sentinel); the war is won or lost at the margins.
3. **Cost.** The war becomes a logistics war fought by psychics as escorts and raiders, not as a decisive arm. Both sides begin to measure victory in mica, not in battles.

---

## Turn 4 — The infantry answer (1940–41): "A psychic is one man; make him spend himself."

1. **Awakening.** Conventional forces learn to fight psychics *without* psychics. A psychic is singular, limited (6–7 Snaps, a ramp-up, 10,000 calories a day), and — critically — **vulnerable to preparation**: an ambush, a denied line of sight, a burst of fire that shatters the barrier.
2. **Response.** **Combined arms.** Magnesium flares blind the Snap's line-of-sight; AT rifles break the barrier at range; piano wire catches low flight; men spread out so there is no massed target to slaughter. The psychic stops being "the army" and becomes **the tip of a conventional spear** — it opens the way, and the infantry, the guns, and the armor do the holding and the killing.
3. **Cost.** This is the *mature* form of psychic war — but it concedes that the psychic's age of invincibility is over. The Ace is no longer a god to be feared; it is a weapon to be used carefully, and used carefully means it is no longer enough by itself.

---

## Turn 5 — The clock (1941–42): "The weapon rots."

1. **Awakening.** The thorium clock and the mica ceiling mean Germany's Aces are **perishable and irreplaceable**. Every Ace is dying on a 6–12 month timer, and none can be replaced — the rock isn't there.
2. **Response.** Doctrine becomes **"win fast or lose."** The Great Refit (captured mica from Suez), the push on Suez itself (the mica stockpiles), the Hollow economy (industrialize the cheap half, at last). Germany converts everything it has left into one final push, because a decaying asset cannot be husbanded — only spent.
3. **Cost.** Germany's entire strategy becomes a countdown. It is no longer fighting to win a war; it is fighting to win *before its weapon dies*. Brilliance cannot outrun a clock.

---

## Turn 6 — Denial inverted (1942): "Use the failure state as a weapon."

1. **Awakening.** Out of Aces, out of mica, Germany faces a wall it cannot break — and it has no psychics left that can Snap through it.
2. **Response.** The **Hollow Storm**: Hollows as psychic batteries, their residual neural energy pumped through mobile Vaults into a single Static-breaking pulse. Germany cracks the wall not with better psychics but with *cheaper failure* — noise was always cheaper than signal, and Hollows are cheaper than Aces. Denial is defeated by denial's own logic.
3. **Cost.** France falls — Paris taken in August 1942, after two and a half years of siege. But Germany spends its psychic reserves and its moral legitimacy to do it, and the world finally sees, in photographs and newsreels, what Germany has become. The victory is hollow in every sense of the word.

---

## Turn 7 — The endgame (1944): "The only winning move is to unmake the power."

1. **Awakening.** After three years, the answer to the psychic is not a better doctrine of fighting — it is to make psychic power itself *impossible*.
2. **Response.** The **EPP bomb** — an Electro-Psychic Pulse that Hollows every psychic within fifty miles and shatters every vacuum tube. It does not fight psychics; it *deletes* them. Mutual assured destruction by technology instead of doctrine.
3. **Cost.** The war ends not with a victory but with a negation — a weapon that un-makes the very power the war was fought over. The question the war opened ("can anyone control this?") is answered with "no — so we will unmake it."

---

## The through-line

The war began with the belief that a psychic was unblockable, and it ends with the proof that everything has a counter — and that the counters were always uglier than the weapon.

| Turn | Thesis | Who paid |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | A psychic is unblockable | (the assumption that starts it all) |
| 1 | Noise is cheaper than signal | France's grid, Britain's dying sentinel |
| 2 | A wall isn't a shield | Germany, forced into siege |
| 3 | Starve the god | Both, in mica and attrition |
| 4 | Make him spend himself | The Ace's invincibility |
| 5 | The weapon rots | Germany, on a countdown |
| 6 | The failure state is a weapon | Germany's soul, France itself |
| 7 | Unmake the power | The psychic age itself |

The sequence is offense → denial → siege → attrition → combined arms → decay → the cheap half → annihilation. Each turn was a response to the last, and each cost more than the one before. The winners were not the brave or the brilliant; they were the patient and the cynical — the ones who could afford to let the weapon rot and then un-make it entirely.
